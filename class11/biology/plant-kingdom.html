CLASS 11 BIOLOGY • CHAPTER 3

Plant Kingdom

Complete notes on major plant groups, their characteristics, life cycles and revision points.

📖 Chapter Overview

The Plant Kingdom includes a wide variety of organisms. Plants are classified into major groups based on characteristics such as body organisation, vascular tissues, seed formation and reproductive structures.

🌿 Major Plant Groups

🟢

Algae

Simple, mostly aquatic organisms with a photosynthetic thallus.

🌱

Bryophytes

Small non-vascular plants that generally grow in moist habitats.

🌿

Pteridophytes

Seedless vascular plants with true roots, stems and leaves.

🌲

Gymnosperms

Seed-producing plants in which seeds are not enclosed within fruits.

🌸

Angiosperms

Flowering plants whose seeds are enclosed within fruits.

🟢 1. Algae

  • Mostly aquatic and photosynthetic.
  • Plant body is generally a simple thallus.
  • They may be unicellular or multicellular.
  • Reproduction can be vegetative, asexual or sexual.
  • Important examples include Chlamydomonas, Volvox, Ulothrix, Spirogyra and Laminaria.

Important Classes

Class Common Name Examples
Chlorophyceae Green algae Chlamydomonas, Volvox, Spirogyra
Phaeophyceae Brown algae Fucus, Laminaria, Sargassum
Rhodophyceae Red algae Polysiphonia, Porphyra, Gelidium

🌱 2. Bryophytes

  • They are generally found in moist and shaded places.
  • They lack true vascular tissues.
  • Plant body may be thalloid or leafy.
  • Water is important for sexual reproduction.
  • The dominant phase is the gametophyte.

Examples

Marchantia, Funaria and Sphagnum.

🌿 3. Pteridophytes

  • They are seedless vascular plants.
  • True roots, stems and leaves are present.
  • Vascular tissues are well developed.
  • They reproduce through spores.
  • The sporophyte is the dominant phase.

Examples

Ferns, Selaginella, Equisetum and Marsilea.

🌲 4. Gymnosperms

  • They are vascular seed-producing plants.
  • Seeds are not enclosed by fruits.
  • Ovules are exposed.
  • They generally form cones.
  • The sporophyte is the dominant plant body.

Examples

Cycas, Pinus and Gnetum.

🌸 5. Angiosperms

  • They are flowering plants.
  • Ovules are enclosed within the ovary.
  • Seeds are enclosed within fruits.
  • They show double fertilisation.
  • They include monocotyledons and dicotyledons.

Monocots

  • One cotyledon
  • Usually parallel venation
  • Fibrous root system is common

Dicots

  • Two cotyledons
  • Reticulate venation is common
  • Tap root system is common

🔄 Alternation of Generations

Plants commonly show alternation between a haploid gametophyte generation and a diploid sporophyte generation.

Gametophyte → Gametes → Fertilisation → Zygote → Sporophyte → Spores → Gametophyte

Three Common Patterns

Haplontic

The haploid phase is dominant and the diploid phase is mainly represented by the zygote.

Diplontic

The diploid sporophyte is the dominant phase.

Haplo-diplontic

Both gametophyte and sporophyte generations are multicellular.

⚡ Quick Revision

  • Algae are mostly aquatic and photosynthetic.
  • Bryophytes are non-vascular plants.
  • Pteridophytes are seedless vascular plants.
  • Gymnosperms have exposed ovules and seeds.
  • Angiosperms are flowering plants.
  • Angiosperms show double fertilisation.
  • Plant life cycles may show alternation of generations.
  • Gametophyte produces gametes.
  • Sporophyte produces spores.

❓ Practice Questions

Q1. Which plant group lacks vascular tissues?

Answer: Bryophytes.

Q2. Which plants are called seedless vascular plants?

Answer: Pteridophytes.

Q3. What is characteristic of gymnosperms?

Answer: Their ovules are exposed and their seeds are not enclosed within fruits.

Q4. Which plant group produces flowers?

Answer: Angiosperms.

Q5. What does the gametophyte produce?

Answer: Gametes.
