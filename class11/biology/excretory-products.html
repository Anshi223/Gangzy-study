🚽 Excretory Products and Elimination

Complete notes covering excretion, nitrogenous wastes, human excretory system, nephron, urine formation, osmoregulation, hormonal regulation and disorders.

📖 Chapter Overview

Excretion is the process by which metabolic waste products are removed from the body. In humans, the kidneys are the major organs responsible for excretion and maintenance of the internal chemical balance of the body.

Excretion → Removal of metabolic wastes from the body

🧪 1. Nitrogenous Wastes

The breakdown of proteins and nucleic acids produces nitrogenous waste products.

Waste Important Point
Ammonia Highly toxic and requires large amounts of water for removal.
Urea Less toxic than ammonia and is the major nitrogenous waste in humans.
Uric acid Relatively less toxic and requires very little water for excretion.

🐟 2. Ammonotelism

Animals that mainly excrete nitrogenous waste in the form of ammonia are called ammonotelic animals.

Ammonia is highly toxic and is generally eliminated where water is readily available.

🧍 3. Ureotelism

Animals that mainly excrete nitrogenous waste in the form of urea are called ureotelic animals.

Humans → Ureotelic

Urea is formed mainly in the liver through the urea cycle. The kidneys then remove urea from the blood.

🐦 4. Uricotelism

Animals that mainly excrete uric acid are called uricotelic animals.

This method conserves water and is especially useful for animals such as birds and many reptiles.

🫘 5. Human Excretory System

The human urinary system consists of:

  • Two kidneys
  • Two ureters
  • Urinary bladder
  • Urethra
Kidneys → Ureters → Urinary bladder → Urethra

🫘 6. Kidneys

Kidneys are a pair of bean-shaped organs located in the abdominal region.

Major functions

  • Remove nitrogenous wastes.
  • Maintain water balance.
  • Maintain electrolyte balance.
  • Help regulate blood pressure.
  • Maintain acid-base balance.
  • Perform endocrine functions.

🔬 7. Nephron

The nephron is the structural and functional unit of the kidney. Each kidney contains a very large number of nephrons.

Main parts

  • Bowman's capsule
  • Glomerulus
  • Proximal convoluted tubule (PCT)
  • Loop of Henle
  • Distal convoluted tubule (DCT)
  • Collecting duct
Glomerulus + Bowman's capsule = Renal corpuscle

🩸 8. Glomerulus

The glomerulus is a network of capillaries present inside Bowman's capsule.

It receives blood through the afferent arteriole and blood leaves through the efferent arteriole.

💧 9. Urine Formation

Urine formation involves three major processes:

  1. Glomerular filtration
  2. Selective reabsorption
  3. Tubular secretion
Filtration → Reabsorption → Secretion → Urine

🔬 10. Glomerular Filtration

Blood is filtered through the glomerulus into Bowman's capsule. The filtrate contains water and many small dissolved substances.

Blood cells and most plasma proteins normally remain in the bloodstream.

♻️ 11. Selective Reabsorption

Useful substances are selectively reabsorbed from the filtrate back into the blood.

The extent of reabsorption varies along different parts of the nephron.

  • Water
  • Glucose
  • Amino acids
  • Useful ions

➕ 12. Tubular Secretion

Certain substances are actively secreted from the blood into the renal tubule.

This helps in maintaining ionic and acid-base balance and eliminating unwanted substances.

  • H⁺
  • K⁺
  • NH₃
  • Other selected substances

🔄 13. Role of PCT

The proximal convoluted tubule is an important site for reabsorption.

  • Large amounts of water are reabsorbed.
  • Glucose is normally reabsorbed.
  • Amino acids are reabsorbed.
  • Important ions are reabsorbed.

↕️ 14. Loop of Henle

The loop of Henle has a descending limb and an ascending limb. It contributes to the mechanism that helps establish the medullary osmotic gradient.

Part General Property
Descending limb More permeable to water.
Ascending limb Relatively impermeable to water and involved in ion transport.

🎯 15. Role of DCT

The distal convoluted tubule participates in selective reabsorption and secretion.

It contributes to the regulation of electrolyte balance and acid-base balance.

💧 16. Collecting Duct

The collecting duct carries urine from nephrons towards the renal pelvis.

Water reabsorption in the collecting duct is regulated, particularly under the influence of antidiuretic hormone.

🧠 17. Counter-Current Mechanism

The counter-current mechanism involving the loop of Henle and vasa recta helps establish and maintain the osmotic gradient in the renal medulla.

Loop of Henle + Vasa recta → Medullary osmotic gradient

This gradient helps the kidneys produce concentrated urine when required.

💧 18. Concentration of Urine

The kidneys can produce concentrated urine by reabsorbing water from the filtrate.

Antidiuretic hormone (ADH) increases water reabsorption in appropriate parts of the nephron and collecting duct.

🧠 19. Regulation by ADH

ADH, also called vasopressin, plays an important role in water balance.

  • Released when the body needs to conserve water.
  • Increases water reabsorption in the kidney.
  • Helps reduce excessive water loss through urine.
ADH ↑ → Water reabsorption ↑ → Urine becomes more concentrated

🧂 20. Regulation by Renin-Angiotensin-Aldosterone System

The kidneys participate in blood pressure and sodium balance through the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS).

Low blood pressure / low renal perfusion → Renin release → Angiotensin II formation → Aldosterone effects → Increased sodium and water retention

🧪 21. Atrial Natriuretic Factor

Atrial natriuretic factor (ANF) is released by the heart when the atria are stretched due to increased blood volume.

ANF promotes sodium and water loss and therefore acts oppositely to mechanisms that increase blood volume.

🚽 22. Micturition

Micturition is the process of expelling urine from the urinary bladder.

Urine stored in the bladder is released through the urethra when the micturition reflex is activated.

🧪 23. Composition of Urine

Normal urine mainly contains:

  • Water
  • Urea
  • Uric acid
  • Creatinine
  • Various ions
Normal urine normally does not contain significant amounts of glucose or proteins.

⚠️ 24. Disorders of the Excretory System

Uraemia

Uraemia refers to accumulation of nitrogenous wastes in the blood due to severe impairment of kidney function.

Renal Failure

Renal failure occurs when the kidneys are unable to perform their functions adequately.

Renal Calculi

Renal calculi, commonly called kidney stones, are solid deposits that can form within the urinary system.

Glomerulonephritis

Glomerulonephritis involves inflammation or damage of the glomeruli.

🏥 25. Dialysis

Dialysis is a medical procedure used to remove wastes and excess fluid from the blood when kidney function is severely impaired.

Dialysis supports waste removal when kidneys cannot perform adequately.

📊 26. Types of Nitrogenous Excretion

Type Main Waste Example
Ammonotelism Ammonia Many aquatic animals
Ureotelism Urea Humans
Uricotelism Uric acid Birds and many reptiles
