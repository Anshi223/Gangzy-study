CLASS 11 BIOLOGY

🌱 Mineral Nutrition

Complete notes on essential mineral elements, their functions, deficiency symptoms, hydroponics, nitrogen metabolism and biological nitrogen fixation.

📖 Chapter Overview

Plants require several inorganic elements for their growth, development and completion of their life cycle. These elements are obtained mainly from the soil, water and atmosphere.

🧂 Mineral Elements

Elements required by plants for normal growth and development.

🌿 Macronutrients

Required by plants in relatively large quantities.

🔬 Micronutrients

Required in very small quantities but are still essential.

1️⃣ Essential Elements

An element is considered essential when a plant cannot complete its life cycle in its absence, its role cannot be replaced by another element and it is directly involved in plant nutrition or metabolism.

⭐ Criteria of Essentiality

  • The element must be absolutely necessary for normal growth and reproduction.
  • Its deficiency prevents the plant from completing its life cycle.
  • The deficiency must be specific to that particular element.
  • The element must have a direct role in plant metabolism.

2️⃣ Macronutrients

Macronutrients are required by plants in relatively large amounts.

Element Major Role
Nitrogen (N) Component of proteins, nucleic acids, chlorophyll and many other compounds.
Phosphorus (P) Part of ATP, nucleic acids, phospholipids and several phosphorylated compounds.
Potassium (K) Helps enzyme activation, stomatal movement and maintenance of cellular conditions.
Calcium (Ca) Important for cell walls, membranes and cell division.
Magnesium (Mg) Central component of chlorophyll and activator of many enzymes.
Sulphur (S) Component of sulphur-containing amino acids and some coenzymes.

3️⃣ Other Important Macronutrients

Carbon

Obtained mainly from atmospheric carbon dioxide and forms the backbone of organic molecules.

Hydrogen

Obtained mainly from water and forms part of many organic compounds.

Oxygen

Required for respiration and is present in many organic compounds.

4️⃣ Micronutrients

Micronutrients are required in very small quantities. Despite being needed in small amounts, they play essential roles in plant metabolism.

Important Micronutrients

  • Iron (Fe)
  • Manganese (Mn)
  • Zinc (Zn)
  • Copper (Cu)
  • Molybdenum (Mo)
  • Boron (B)
  • Chlorine (Cl)
  • Nickel (Ni)

5️⃣ Functions of Important Micronutrients

Element Important Function
Iron (Fe) Important in electron transport and synthesis of certain cellular components.
Manganese (Mn) Acts as an enzyme activator and is involved in photosynthetic processes.
Zinc (Zn) Activates several enzymes and is involved in hormone metabolism.
Copper (Cu) Involved in redox reactions and electron transport.
Molybdenum (Mo) Important for nitrate reduction and nitrogen metabolism.
Boron (B) Important for cell wall development, growth and reproductive processes.
Chlorine (Cl) Participates in photosynthetic processes and ionic balance.
Nickel (Ni) Required for the activity of the enzyme urease.

6️⃣ Mineral Deficiency

When a plant does not receive an essential mineral in sufficient quantity, characteristic deficiency symptoms may appear.

🍃 Chlorosis

Yellowing of leaves due to loss or reduced formation of chlorophyll.

🌿 Necrosis

Death of cells or tissues, often resulting in brown or dead patches.

🌱 Stunted Growth

Reduction in normal growth of roots, stems or leaves.

🍂 Premature Leaf Fall

Leaves may fall earlier than normal under certain nutrient deficiencies.

7️⃣ Mobile and Immobile Elements

Mobile Elements

Some nutrients can be redistributed from older tissues to younger growing tissues. Therefore, deficiency symptoms often appear first in older leaves.

Examples include nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium and magnesium.

Immobile Elements

Some nutrients are less readily redistributed within the plant. Their deficiency symptoms commonly appear first in younger tissues.

Examples include calcium and boron.

8️⃣ Hydroponics

Hydroponics is the technique of growing plants in a nutrient solution without soil.

🌱 Importance of Hydroponics

  • Helps study mineral requirements of plants.
  • Allows controlled supply of nutrients.
  • Useful for identifying essential elements.
  • Can be used for crop production under controlled conditions.

9️⃣ Nitrogen

Nitrogen is one of the most important mineral nutrients required by plants.

Functions of Nitrogen

  • Component of amino acids.
  • Required for protein synthesis.
  • Component of nucleic acids.
  • Required for chlorophyll formation.
  • Present in several important metabolic compounds.

🔟 Nitrogen Cycle

Atmospheric nitrogen is not directly usable by most plants. It is converted into usable nitrogen compounds through various biological and chemical processes.

Basic Flow

Atmospheric N₂ ↓ Nitrogen Fixation ↓ Ammonia / Ammonium Compounds ↓ Nitrites ↓ Nitrates ↓ Plant Uptake ↓ Animals ↓ Decomposition ↓ Nitrogen Compounds

1️⃣1️⃣ Biological Nitrogen Fixation

Biological nitrogen fixation is the conversion of atmospheric nitrogen into biologically useful nitrogen compounds by certain microorganisms.

🦠 Rhizobium

Rhizobium bacteria live symbiotically in the root nodules of leguminous plants and help in nitrogen fixation.

🌱 Root Nodules

Root nodules are specialised structures formed on roots of many leguminous plants through association with nitrogen-fixing bacteria.

1️⃣2️⃣ Nitrogenase

Nitrogenase is the enzyme complex responsible for biological nitrogen fixation.

  • It is sensitive to oxygen.
  • Biological nitrogen fixation requires energy.
  • Reducing power is required for the process.
  • Specialised structures help maintain suitable conditions in nitrogen-fixing organisms.

1️⃣3️⃣ Denitrification

Denitrification is the conversion of nitrates into gaseous nitrogen compounds by certain microorganisms, returning nitrogen to the atmosphere.

Nitrates → Nitrogenous gases → Atmosphere
