Biomolecules

Complete notes on carbohydrates, proteins, lipids, nucleic acids, enzymes and other important biomolecules.

📖 CHAPTER OVERVIEW

Biomolecules

Living organisms contain a wide variety of chemical compounds. Many of these compounds are called biomolecules because they are associated with living systems.

Major biomolecules include carbohydrates, proteins, lipids and nucleic acids.

🧪 CONCEPT 1

Biomolecules and Chemical Analysis

Chemical analysis helps identify and study the different compounds present in living organisms.

Biomolecules can be broadly grouped into organic and inorganic substances.

🍚 BIOMOLECULE 1

Carbohydrates

Carbohydrates are organic compounds generally composed of carbon, hydrogen and oxygen.

Functions

  • Major source of energy.
  • Energy storage.
  • Structural roles in organisms.
🍬 CARBOHYDRATES

Types of Carbohydrates

Monosaccharides

Simple sugars that cannot be hydrolysed into smaller carbohydrate units.

Examples: glucose, fructose and ribose.

Disaccharides

Formed by the combination of two monosaccharides.

Examples: sucrose, maltose and lactose.

Polysaccharides

Large carbohydrates made of many monosaccharide units.

Examples: starch, glycogen and cellulose.

⭐ IMPORTANT

Important Carbohydrates

Glucose

An important monosaccharide and major cellular energy source.

Fructose

A monosaccharide commonly found in fruits and other natural sources.

Sucrose

A disaccharide made of glucose and fructose.

Starch

An important storage polysaccharide in plants.

Glycogen

An important storage polysaccharide in animals and fungi.

Cellulose

A structural polysaccharide forming an important component of plant cell walls.

🥚 BIOMOLECULE 2

Proteins

Proteins are polymers made up of amino acids. They perform many structural and functional roles in living organisms.

Functions

  • Structural support.
  • Enzymatic activity.
  • Transport.
  • Movement.
  • Defence.
  • Cell signalling and regulation.
🧬 PROTEINS

Amino Acids

Amino acids are the basic units of proteins. They contain amino and carboxyl groups.

General Structure

  • Amino group
  • Carboxyl group
  • Hydrogen atom
  • Variable R group

The R group determines the characteristics of an amino acid.

🔗 PROTEIN STRUCTURE

Peptide Bond

Amino acids are joined together by peptide bonds to form polypeptide chains.

A protein may consist of one or more polypeptide chains folded into a specific three-dimensional structure.

🏗️ PROTEIN STRUCTURE

Levels of Protein Organisation

Primary Structure

The specific sequence of amino acids in a polypeptide chain.

Secondary Structure

Local folding patterns such as alpha-helices and beta-sheets.

Tertiary Structure

Overall three-dimensional folding of a polypeptide chain.

Quaternary Structure

Organisation of multiple polypeptide chains in a functional protein.

🧈 BIOMOLECULE 3

Lipids

Lipids are a diverse group of water-insoluble organic compounds.

Functions

  • Energy storage.
  • Formation of biological membranes.
  • Insulation and protection.
  • Participation in signalling.
🧈 LIPIDS

Fats and Oils

Fats

Often contain a higher proportion of saturated fatty acids and are generally solid at room temperature.

Oils

Often contain a higher proportion of unsaturated fatty acids and are generally liquid at room temperature.

🧬 BIOMOLECULE 4

Nucleic Acids

Nucleic acids are polymers of nucleotides. The two major types are DNA and RNA.

Functions

  • Storage of genetic information.
  • Transmission of hereditary information.
  • Participation in protein synthesis.
🧬 NUCLEIC ACIDS

Nucleotide

A nucleotide consists of three components:

  • Nitrogenous base
  • Pentose sugar
  • Phosphate group

A nucleoside contains a nitrogenous base and pentose sugar but lacks the phosphate group.

📊 COMPARISON

DNA vs RNA

Feature DNA RNA
Sugar Deoxyribose Ribose
Bases A, G, C, T A, G, C, U
Typical structure Double-stranded Usually single-stranded
Main role Genetic information storage Multiple roles including protein synthesis
⚙️ BIOMOLECULE 5

Enzymes

Enzymes are biological catalysts that increase the rate of biochemical reactions without being consumed in the reaction.

Important Features

  • Highly specific in action.
  • Usually proteinaceous in nature.
  • Work efficiently under suitable conditions.
  • Lower the activation energy of reactions.
⚡ ENZYME ACTIVITY

Factors Affecting Enzyme Activity

Temperature

Enzyme activity changes with temperature. Excessive heat may denature many enzymes.

pH

Each enzyme generally has an optimum pH range for maximum activity.

Substrate Concentration

Increasing substrate concentration can increase reaction rate until enzyme active sites become effectively saturated.

Inhibitors

Certain substances can reduce enzyme activity by interfering with enzyme action.

⚙️ ENZYMES

Cofactors

Some enzymes require non-protein components called cofactors for their activity.

  • Metal ions
  • Coenzymes
  • Prosthetic groups
🔄 METABOLISM

Metabolism

Metabolism refers to the sum of all chemical reactions occurring within a living organism.

Two Major Components

  • Anabolism: Building of complex molecules from simpler molecules.
  • Catabolism: Breakdown of complex molecules into simpler molecules.
🌿 IMPORTANT

Primary and Secondary Metabolites

Primary Metabolites

Directly involved in normal growth, development and reproduction.

Examples include amino acids, sugars and organic acids.

Secondary Metabolites

Compounds produced by organisms that often have specialised ecological or biological roles.

Examples include pigments, alkaloids and essential oils.

📝 PRACTICE

Important Questions

Q1. What are biomolecules?

Chemical compounds associated with living organisms.

Q2. What are proteins made of?

Proteins are made of amino acids linked by peptide bonds.

Q3. What are the components of a nucleotide?

Nitrogenous base, pentose sugar and phosphate group.

Q4. What is the function of an enzyme?

An enzyme acts as a biological catalyst and increases the rate of a biochemical reaction.

Q5. Differentiate between anabolism and catabolism.

Anabolism builds complex molecules, while catabolism breaks complex molecules into simpler ones.
