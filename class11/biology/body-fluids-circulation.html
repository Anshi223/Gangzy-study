Class 11 Biology

🫀 Body Fluids and Circulation

Complete notes covering blood, lymph, blood groups, coagulation, human heart, cardiac cycle, ECG, double circulation and important cardiovascular disorders.

📖 Chapter Overview

The circulatory system transports oxygen, nutrients, hormones and other substances throughout the body and carries metabolic wastes to organs where they can be removed.

Human circulatory system = Blood + Heart + Blood vessels

🩸 1. Body Fluids

The major body fluids involved in transport are blood and lymph. They help maintain communication and exchange between different parts of the body.

  • Blood
  • Lymph

🩸 2. Blood

Blood is a specialised connective tissue consisting of a fluid matrix called plasma and formed elements.

Major components

  • Plasma
  • Red blood cells (RBCs)
  • White blood cells (WBCs)
  • Platelets

💧 3. Plasma

Plasma is the fluid component of blood in which blood cells and other substances are suspended.

  • Contains water as its major component.
  • Contains proteins.
  • Transports nutrients and hormones.
  • Carries metabolic wastes.
  • Helps maintain osmotic balance and pH.

🔴 4. Red Blood Cells

Red blood cells, or erythrocytes, are specialised cells that transport oxygen mainly through haemoglobin.

  • They are biconcave in shape.
  • Mature human RBCs lack a nucleus.
  • They contain haemoglobin.
  • They transport oxygen and contribute to CO₂ transport.
RBC + Haemoglobin → Major oxygen transport system

⚪ 5. White Blood Cells

White blood cells are involved mainly in defence and immunity.

Type Important Role
Neutrophils Phagocytosis and defence
Lymphocytes Specific immune responses
Monocytes Phagocytosis
Eosinophils Allergic responses and defence against parasites
Basophils Release substances involved in inflammatory responses

🟡 6. Platelets

Platelets, also called thrombocytes, are cell fragments important in blood clotting.

  • Help form blood clots.
  • Reduce blood loss after injury.
  • Participate in the coagulation process.

🩹 7. Blood Coagulation

Blood coagulation is the process by which blood forms a clot at the site of a damaged blood vessel.

Injury → Platelet activation → Coagulation reactions → Fibrin clot

Fibrin forms a network that traps blood cells and helps strengthen the clot.

🧬 8. Blood Groups

The ABO blood group system is based mainly on the presence or absence of A and B antigens on red blood cells.

Blood Group Antigen on RBC Antibody in Plasma
A A Anti-B
B B Anti-A
AB A and B Neither anti-A nor anti-B
O Neither A nor B Anti-A and Anti-B
For safe transfusion, blood group compatibility must be carefully matched; emergency transfusions should follow medical protocols.

➕ 9. Rh Blood Group

The Rh system is another important blood group system. The presence of the Rh antigen, especially the D antigen, is commonly represented as Rh-positive.

A person lacking the D antigen is generally described as Rh-negative.

🤰 10. Rh Incompatibility

Rh incompatibility can become clinically important during pregnancy when an Rh-negative mother carries an Rh-positive foetus.

Medical management and preventive treatment can greatly reduce the risk of complications.

💧 11. Lymph

Lymph is a colourless fluid formed from tissue fluid and circulates through lymphatic vessels.

Functions

  • Returns excess tissue fluid to the blood.
  • Helps transport absorbed fats from the intestine.
  • Participates in immune defence.

🫀 12. Human Heart

The human heart is a muscular organ that pumps blood throughout the body.

Four chambers

  • Right atrium
  • Right ventricle
  • Left atrium
  • Left ventricle
Right side → Mainly handles deoxygenated blood
Left side → Mainly handles oxygenated blood

🚪 13. Valves of the Heart

Heart valves prevent the backward flow of blood.

Valve Location
Tricuspid valve Right atrium and right ventricle
Bicuspid/Mitral valve Left atrium and left ventricle
Pulmonary semilunar valve Right ventricle and pulmonary artery
Aortic semilunar valve Left ventricle and aorta

🩸 14. Major Blood Vessels

Blood Vessel Function
Aorta Carries oxygenated blood from left ventricle to body.
Pulmonary artery Carries deoxygenated blood from right ventricle to lungs.
Pulmonary veins Carry oxygenated blood from lungs to left atrium.
Venae cavae Return deoxygenated blood from body to right atrium.

🔄 15. Cardiac Cycle

The cardiac cycle represents the sequence of events occurring during one complete heartbeat.

Main phases

  • Atrial systole
  • Ventricular systole
  • Joint diastole
Systole → Contraction
Diastole → Relaxation

💓 16. Heartbeat

The rhythmic contraction and relaxation of the heart produces the heartbeat.

The cardiac cycle involves coordinated movement of blood through the chambers and vessels.

⚡ 17. Conducting System of Heart

The heart has a specialised conducting system that generates and spreads electrical impulses.

  • Sinoatrial node (SA node)
  • Atrioventricular node (AV node)
  • Atrioventricular bundle
  • Bundle branches
  • Purkinje fibres
SA node → Natural pacemaker of the heart

📈 18. Electrocardiogram (ECG)

An electrocardiogram records the electrical activity of the heart during a cardiac cycle.

Major waves

Wave Represents
P wave Atrial depolarisation
QRS complex Ventricular depolarisation
T wave Ventricular repolarisation

🔁 19. Double Circulation

In humans, blood passes through the heart twice during one complete circulation.

Pulmonary circulation

Right ventricle → Pulmonary artery → Lungs → Pulmonary veins → Left atrium

Systemic circulation

Left ventricle → Aorta → Body tissues → Venae cavae → Right atrium

Double circulation keeps oxygenated and deoxygenated blood largely separated in humans.

🩸 20. Blood Vessels

Vessel Main Feature
Arteries Carry blood away from the heart; generally thick-walled.
Veins Carry blood towards the heart; valves may prevent backflow.
Capillaries Very thin vessels where exchange occurs.
Exception: Pulmonary artery carries deoxygenated blood, while pulmonary veins carry oxygenated blood.

⚠️ 21. Cardiovascular Disorders

Hypertension

Hypertension refers to persistently elevated blood pressure and requires proper medical evaluation and management.

Coronary Artery Disease

Coronary artery disease involves narrowing or blockage of coronary arteries, reducing blood supply to heart muscle.

Angina

Angina is chest discomfort caused by inadequate oxygen supply to the heart muscle.

Heart Failure

Heart failure occurs when the heart is unable to pump blood adequately to meet the body's needs.

📊 22. Arteries vs Veins

Feature Arteries Veins
Direction Away from heart Towards heart
Walls Generally thick and elastic Generally thinner
Valves Generally absent Usually present in many veins
Pressure Higher Lower
