CLASS 11 BIOLOGY

🌿 Photosynthesis in Higher Plants

Complete chapter notes covering pigments, light reaction, photophosphorylation, Calvin cycle, C4 pathway, photorespiration and factors affecting photosynthesis.

📖 Chapter Overview

Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants use light energy to synthesize carbohydrates from carbon dioxide and water. Oxygen is released as a by-product of the process.

⭐ Overall Equation

6CO₂ + 6H₂O + Light Energy → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂

☀️ Light Reaction

Takes place in the thylakoid membranes and converts light energy into chemical energy.

🌱 Calvin Cycle

Takes place in the chloroplast stroma and fixes carbon dioxide.

🍃 Chloroplast

The main photosynthetic organelle of green plants.

1️⃣ Site of Photosynthesis

Photosynthesis mainly occurs in the leaves. The chloroplast is the major organelle responsible for photosynthesis.

Chloroplast Structure

  • Outer membrane
  • Inner membrane
  • Stroma
  • Thylakoids
  • Grana
  • Stroma lamellae

The light-dependent reactions occur mainly on the thylakoid membranes, while carbon fixation reactions occur in the stroma.

2️⃣ Photosynthetic Pigments

Photosynthetic pigments absorb light energy and transfer it to the reaction centres of photosystems.

Pigment Role
Chlorophyll a Main reaction-centre pigment of photosynthesis.
Chlorophyll b Accessory pigment that helps capture light.
Carotenoids Accessory pigments that absorb additional wavelengths and provide photoprotection.

3️⃣ Photosystems

Photosystems are functional units present in the thylakoid membranes that participate in light absorption and electron transfer.

Photosystem I (PS I)

Its reaction centre is associated with P700.

Photosystem II (PS II)

Its reaction centre is associated with P680.

4️⃣ Light Reaction

Light reactions occur in the thylakoid membranes of chloroplasts. Light energy is converted into chemical energy in the form of ATP and NADPH.

Main Events

  1. Light is absorbed by photosynthetic pigments.
  2. Electrons are excited and transferred through electron carriers.
  3. Water is split during photolysis.
  4. Oxygen is released as a by-product.
  5. ATP is produced through photophosphorylation.
  6. NADPH is formed.

5️⃣ Photolysis of Water

In photosystem II, light-driven processes are associated with splitting of water molecules.

Products

  • Electrons
  • Protons (H⁺)
  • Oxygen (O₂)

The oxygen released during photosynthesis comes from water.

6️⃣ Electron Transport Chain

Excited electrons move through a series of electron carriers. Their movement contributes to the formation of a proton gradient across the thylakoid membrane.

Importance

The proton gradient drives ATP synthesis through the enzyme ATP synthase.

7️⃣ Photophosphorylation

Photophosphorylation is the formation of ATP from ADP and inorganic phosphate using light energy.

🔄 Non-cyclic Photophosphorylation

  • Involves both PS I and PS II.
  • Electrons do not return to the original chlorophyll molecule.
  • Produces ATP.
  • Produces NADPH.
  • Oxygen is released.

♻️ Cyclic Photophosphorylation

  • Mainly involves PS I.
  • Electrons return to the reaction centre.
  • Produces ATP.
  • Does not produce NADPH.
  • Does not release oxygen.

8️⃣ Chemiosmotic Hypothesis

ATP synthesis during the light reaction is explained by the chemiosmotic hypothesis.

Sequence

  1. Electron transport causes accumulation of protons in the thylakoid lumen.
  2. A proton gradient develops across the thylakoid membrane.
  3. Protons move back through ATP synthase.
  4. ATP is formed from ADP and inorganic phosphate.

9️⃣ Calvin Cycle

The Calvin cycle is a series of reactions through which carbon dioxide is fixed and converted into carbohydrate molecules. It occurs in the chloroplast stroma.

Three Main Stages

  1. Carboxylation – CO₂ is fixed to RuBP.
  2. Reduction – the products are reduced using ATP and NADPH.
  3. Regeneration – RuBP is regenerated.

🔟 RuBisCO

RuBisCO is the enzyme responsible for catalysing the fixation of carbon dioxide to RuBP during the Calvin cycle.

Full Form

Ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate carboxylase/oxygenase

RuBisCO can act as both a carboxylase and an oxygenase, which is important in understanding photorespiration.

1️⃣1️⃣ C4 Pathway

Some plants have a specialised carbon fixation pathway called the C4 pathway. It helps plants efficiently fix carbon dioxide under conditions such as high light intensity and temperature.

Examples

Maize and sugarcane are examples of C4 plants.

Important Features

  • Initial CO₂ fixation occurs in mesophyll cells.
  • PEP carboxylase participates in initial fixation.
  • Four-carbon compounds are formed.
  • Calvin cycle occurs in bundle sheath cells.
  • Kranz anatomy is associated with C4 plants.

1️⃣2️⃣ C3 vs C4 Plants

Feature C3 Plants C4 Plants
First stable product 3-carbon compound 4-carbon compound
Initial CO₂ fixation enzyme RuBisCO PEP carboxylase
Kranz anatomy Usually absent Present
Photorespiration Significant Very low/negligible under typical conditions
Examples Wheat, rice Maize, sugarcane

1️⃣3️⃣ Photorespiration

Photorespiration is a process associated with the oxygenase activity of RuBisCO. It becomes more prominent when the concentration of oxygen is relatively high compared with carbon dioxide.

Important Points

  • RuBisCO can bind oxygen instead of carbon dioxide.
  • It reduces photosynthetic efficiency.
  • It is more prominent in C3 plants.
  • C4 plants largely minimise photorespiration by concentrating CO₂ around RuBisCO.

1️⃣4️⃣ Factors Affecting Photosynthesis

☀️ Light

Light intensity, quality and duration can affect the rate of photosynthesis.

🌡️ Temperature

Photosynthesis is controlled by enzyme activity and therefore temperature affects its rate.

🌬️ Carbon Dioxide

CO₂ concentration can influence the rate of carbon fixation.

💧 Water

Water availability can indirectly affect photosynthesis, including through effects on stomatal opening.

1️⃣5️⃣ Law of Limiting Factors

When several factors affect a process, the rate may be limited by the factor that is closest to its minimum requirement.

Example

If sufficient light and water are available but CO₂ is low, increasing CO₂ may increase the rate of photosynthesis until another factor becomes limiting.

  • Chloroplasts are the main site of photosynthesis.
  • Chlorophyll a is the main reaction-centre pigment.
  • PS I has P700 as its reaction centre.
  • PS II has P680 as its reaction centre.
  • Light reactions occur mainly in thylakoid membranes.
  • Calvin cycle occurs in the chloroplast stroma.
  • Photolysis of water is associated with PS II.
  • Non-cyclic photophosphorylation produces ATP and NADPH and releases oxygen.
  • Cyclic photophosphorylation mainly produces ATP.
  • RuBisCO fixes CO₂ during the Calvin cycle.
  • C4 plants show Kranz anatomy.
  • Maize and sugarcane are C4 plants.
  • Photorespiration is associated with the oxygenase activity of RuBisCO.
