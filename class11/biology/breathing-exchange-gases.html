Class 11 Biology

🫁 Breathing and Exchange of Gases

Complete notes covering the human respiratory system, mechanism of breathing, exchange and transport of gases, regulation of respiration and respiratory disorders.

📖 Chapter Overview

Breathing is the process by which oxygen is taken into the body and carbon dioxide is removed. Cellular respiration uses oxygen to release energy from food, while carbon dioxide is produced as a metabolic waste.

Breathing → Exchange of gases → Cellular respiration → Energy

🫁 1. Respiratory System

The human respiratory system consists of organs and structures that conduct air and provide a surface for gaseous exchange.

Main parts

  • Nostrils
  • Nasal passage
  • Pharynx
  • Larynx
  • Trachea
  • Bronchi
  • Bronchioles
  • Alveoli
  • Lungs
Nostrils → Nasal passage → Pharynx → Larynx → Trachea → Bronchi → Bronchioles → Alveoli

👃 2. Nasal Passage

Air enters through the nostrils and passes through the nasal passages.

  • Filters incoming air.
  • Helps warm the air.
  • Adds moisture to inhaled air.

🗣️ 3. Pharynx and Larynx

Pharynx

The pharynx is a common passage for food and air.

Larynx

The larynx is commonly called the voice box and contains the vocal cords.

Larynx → Voice production

🫧 4. Trachea and Bronchi

The trachea is a tube that carries air towards the lungs. It divides into two primary bronchi.

The bronchi further divide into smaller bronchioles inside the lungs.

🫧 5. Alveoli

Alveoli are tiny air-filled sacs present at the ends of bronchioles and form the major site of gaseous exchange.

Features

  • Large surface area.
  • Thin walls.
  • Rich blood supply.
  • Close association with blood capillaries.
Alveoli → Main site of O₂ and CO₂ exchange

🫁 6. Lungs and Pleura

Humans have a pair of lungs located in the thoracic cavity. The lungs are covered by a double-layered membrane called pleura.

Pleural fluid between the layers helps reduce friction during lung movement.

💨 7. Mechanism of Breathing

Breathing consists of two major processes:

  • Inspiration
  • Expiration

⬇️ 8. Inspiration

Inspiration is the process of taking air into the lungs.

During inspiration

  • Diaphragm contracts and flattens.
  • External intercostal muscles contract.
  • Volume of thoracic cavity increases.
  • Pressure inside the lungs decreases.
  • Air enters the lungs.
Thoracic volume ↑ → Lung pressure ↓ → Air enters

⬆️ 9. Expiration

Expiration is the process by which air is expelled from the lungs.

During normal expiration

  • Diaphragm relaxes.
  • Thoracic volume decreases.
  • Pressure inside the lungs increases.
  • Air moves out of the lungs.
Thoracic volume ↓ → Lung pressure ↑ → Air moves out

📊 10. Respiratory Volumes

Volume Meaning
Tidal Volume (TV) Air inspired or expired during normal breathing.
Inspiratory Reserve Volume (IRV) Additional air that can be inspired after normal inspiration.
Expiratory Reserve Volume (ERV) Additional air that can be expired after normal expiration.
Residual Volume (RV) Air remaining in lungs after forceful expiration.

🧮 11. Respiratory Capacities

Capacity Formula
Inspiratory Capacity TV + IRV
Expiratory Capacity TV + ERV
Functional Residual Capacity ERV + RV
Vital Capacity IRV + TV + ERV
Total Lung Capacity IRV + TV + ERV + RV

🔬 12. Exchange of Gases

Exchange of oxygen and carbon dioxide occurs by diffusion across respiratory surfaces.

In humans, gaseous exchange occurs mainly across the thin alveolar-capillary membrane.

Alveoli ↔ Blood capillaries

🩸 13. Transport of Oxygen

Oxygen is transported in blood mainly by haemoglobin present inside red blood cells.

A small amount of oxygen is transported dissolved in plasma.

O₂ + Haemoglobin ⇌ Oxyhaemoglobin

🩸 14. Oxygen-Haemoglobin Dissociation

Haemoglobin binds oxygen reversibly. The affinity of haemoglobin for oxygen changes depending on conditions in different tissues.

In tissues where oxygen concentration is lower and carbon dioxide concentration is relatively higher, oxygen is released more readily.

🌫️ 15. Transport of Carbon Dioxide

Carbon dioxide is transported in blood in three major forms.

Form Approximate Contribution
Dissolved in plasma Small amount
As bicarbonate ions Major portion
As carbaminohaemoglobin Significant portion

⚗️ 16. Bicarbonate Formation

Inside red blood cells, carbon dioxide reacts with water. The enzyme carbonic anhydrase greatly speeds up this reaction.

CO₂ + H₂O ⇌ H₂CO₃ ⇌ H⁺ + HCO₃⁻

Bicarbonate ions are an important form in which carbon dioxide is transported in blood.

🧠 17. Regulation of Respiration

Respiration is regulated by nervous and chemical mechanisms. The respiratory centres in the brain help control breathing.

Changes in carbon dioxide concentration and hydrogen ion concentration are important signals for regulation of breathing.

🧠 18. Respiratory Centres

  • Respiratory rhythm is controlled by centres in the brainstem.
  • The medulla contains important respiratory control centres.
  • The pons contains centres that influence the respiratory rhythm.
  • Chemoreceptors help detect changes related to CO₂ and H⁺.

🏃 19. Breathing During Exercise

During exercise, the body's demand for oxygen increases and more carbon dioxide is produced.

As a result, breathing becomes faster and deeper to increase gas exchange.

⚠️ 20. Respiratory Disorders

Asthma

Asthma is associated with narrowing and inflammation of airways and can cause difficulty in breathing.

Emphysema

Emphysema involves damage to alveolar walls, reducing the surface available for gaseous exchange. Smoking is an important risk factor.

Occupational Respiratory Disorders

Long-term exposure to certain dusts and pollutants at workplaces can cause respiratory problems.

📊 21. Inspiration vs Expiration

Feature Inspiration Expiration
Air movement Into lungs Out of lungs
Diaphragm Contracts Relaxes
Thoracic volume Increases Decreases
Lung pressure Decreases Increases

  • Diaphragm plays a major role in breathing.

  • Carbon dioxide is transported mainly as bicarbonate ions.
  • Carbonic anhydrase catalyses bicarbonate formation.
  • Medulla and pons participate in respiratory regulation.
  • TV = Tidal Volume.
  • VC = IRV + TV + ERV.
  • TLC = IRV + TV + ERV + RV.
  • Asthma affects the airways.
