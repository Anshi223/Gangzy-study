CLASS 11 BIOLOGY • CHAPTER 2

Biological Classification

Complete chapter notes, five kingdom classification, important characteristics and practice questions.

📖 Chapter Overview

Biological classification is the scientific arrangement of organisms into groups based on their similarities, differences and evolutionary relationships.

🔬 Classification Systems

Two Kingdom Classification

Organisms were traditionally divided into Plantae and Animalia.

Five Kingdom Classification

R.H. Whittaker proposed the five kingdom classification in 1969.

🌍 Five Kingdom Classification

🦠

Monera

Includes prokaryotic organisms such as bacteria and cyanobacteria.

🧫

Protista

Mostly unicellular eukaryotic organisms, including many protozoans and algae.

🍄

Fungi

Eukaryotic organisms generally having chitinous cell walls and absorptive nutrition.

🌱

Plantae

Multicellular eukaryotic organisms, generally photosynthetic with cellulose cell walls.

🐾

Animalia

Multicellular eukaryotic organisms that obtain food by ingestion.

🦠 Kingdom Monera

  • Organisms are prokaryotic.
  • Usually unicellular.
  • Cell wall is generally present.
  • Membrane-bound organelles are absent.
  • Examples include bacteria and cyanobacteria.

Important Groups

Archaebacteria

They can live in extreme environments. Examples include methanogens, halophiles and thermoacidophiles.

Eubacteria

Common bacteria with diverse nutritional and ecological roles.

🧫 Kingdom Protista

  • Mostly unicellular eukaryotic organisms.
  • Mostly aquatic.
  • Some are autotrophic while others are heterotrophic.
  • Reproduction may be sexual or asexual.

Examples

Amoeba, Paramecium, Euglena and diatoms are examples of organisms traditionally placed in Protista.

🍄 Kingdom Fungi

  • Fungi are eukaryotic organisms.
  • Most are multicellular; yeast is unicellular.
  • Cell wall is mainly made of chitin.
  • They are heterotrophic and generally absorptive in nutrition.
  • Many fungi reproduce through spores.

Examples

Rhizopus, yeast, mushrooms and Penicillium.

🌱 Kingdom Plantae

  • Multicellular eukaryotic organisms.
  • Cell wall is mainly composed of cellulose.
  • Most are photosynthetic.
  • Chlorophyll is commonly present in photosynthetic members.

🐾 Kingdom Animalia

  • Multicellular eukaryotic organisms.
  • Cell wall is absent.
  • Heterotrophic and generally ingestive in nutrition.
  • Most animals are capable of movement.

🧬 Viruses, Viroids and Lichens

Viruses

Viruses are acellular infectious entities that reproduce only inside suitable living host cells.

Viroids

Viroids are very small infectious agents made primarily of RNA and lacking a protein coat.

Lichens

Lichens represent a symbiotic association between a fungus and a photosynthetic partner such as an alga or cyanobacterium.

📊 Quick Comparison

Plantae Eukaryotic Multicellular Mostly autotrophic

⚡ Quick Revision

  • R.H. Whittaker proposed five kingdom classification in 1969.
  • Monera contains prokaryotic organisms.
  • Protista mainly contains unicellular eukaryotes.
  • Fungi generally have chitinous cell walls.
  • Plant cells generally have cellulose cell walls.
  • Animal cells do not have cell walls.
  • Viruses are acellular infectious entities.
  • Lichens show a symbiotic association.

❓ Practice Questions

Q1. Who proposed the five kingdom classification?

Answer: R.H. Whittaker.

Q2. Which kingdom contains prokaryotic organisms?

Answer: Kingdom Monera.

Q3. What is the major component of the fungal cell wall?

Answer: Chitin.

Q4. What are lichens?

Answer: Lichens are a symbiotic association between a fungus and a photosynthetic partner such as an alga or cyanobacterium.
