Cell: The Unit of Life

Complete chapter notes covering cell theory, prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells, cell organelles and their functions.

📖 CHAPTER OVERVIEW

Cell: The Unit of Life

The cell is the basic structural and functional unit of life. All living organisms are made up of cells or cell products.

Cells may be broadly classified into prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells.

📚 CONCEPT 1

Cell Theory

  • All living organisms are composed of cells.
  • The cell is the basic structural and functional unit of life.
  • New cells arise from pre-existing cells.

The cell theory was developed through the contributions of scientists including Schleiden and Schwann, and later modified by Virchow.

🦠 CELL TYPE 1

Prokaryotic Cell

Nucleoid

Genetic material is present in a region called the nucleoid and is not surrounded by a nuclear membrane.

Cell Wall

Provides protection and maintains the shape of the cell.

Ribosomes

Small ribosomes are involved in protein synthesis.

Plasma Membrane

Forms the selective boundary of the cell.

🌱 CELL TYPE 2

Eukaryotic Cell

Eukaryotic cells have a well-defined nucleus surrounded by a nuclear membrane and contain membrane-bound organelles.

  • Plant cells
  • Animal cells
  • Fungal cells
  • Protist cells
🧫 ORGANELLE 1

Plasma Membrane

The plasma membrane forms the outer boundary of the cell and separates the cell contents from the surrounding environment.

Functions

  • Maintains cell boundary.
  • Controls movement of substances.
  • Helps in cell communication.
  • Participates in endocytosis and exocytosis.

The membrane is described by the fluid mosaic model.

🌿 ORGANELLE 2

Cell Wall

The cell wall is present outside the plasma membrane in plant cells.

  • Provides mechanical support.
  • Protects the cell.
  • Helps maintain cell shape.
  • Prevents excessive expansion of the cell.

The primary cell wall mainly contains cellulose, hemicellulose and pectin.

🔄 ORGANELLE GROUP

Endomembrane System

Endoplasmic Reticulum

Forms a network of membranes involved in synthesis and transport of substances.

Golgi Apparatus

Modifies, packages and transports proteins and other materials.

Lysosomes

Membrane-bound vesicles containing digestive enzymes.

Vacuoles

Storage compartments containing cell sap and other substances.

🧬 ORGANELLE

Endoplasmic Reticulum

Rough ER

Has ribosomes attached to its surface and is mainly associated with protein synthesis.

Smooth ER

Lacks ribosomes and is involved in lipid synthesis and other cellular functions.

📦 ORGANELLE

Golgi Apparatus

The Golgi apparatus consists of flattened membrane-bound sacs called cisternae.

Functions

  • Modification of proteins and lipids.
  • Packaging of materials.
  • Formation of secretory vesicles.
  • Formation of lysosomes.
♻️ ORGANELLE

Lysosomes

Lysosomes contain hydrolytic enzymes that help digest cellular materials.

  • Intracellular digestion.
  • Removal of worn-out cell components.
  • Breakdown of cellular waste.
⚡ ORGANELLE

Mitochondria

Mitochondria are double-membrane-bound organelles mainly involved in aerobic respiration and ATP production.

Structure

  • Outer membrane
  • Inner membrane
  • Intermembrane space
  • Matrix
  • Cristae

Mitochondria contain their own DNA and ribosomes.

🌱 ORGANELLE

Plastids

Chloroplast

Contains chlorophyll and is the major site of photosynthesis in plants.

Chromoplast

Contains coloured pigments other than chlorophyll.

Leucoplast

Colourless plastids mainly involved in storage.

☀️ CHLOROPLAST

Structure of Chloroplast

  • Outer membrane
  • Inner membrane
  • Stroma
  • Thylakoids
  • Grana
  • Stroma lamellae

Chloroplasts also contain their own DNA and ribosomes.

🧬 ORGANELLE

Ribosomes

Ribosomes are non-membrane-bound structures responsible for protein synthesis.

  • Prokaryotic ribosome → 70S
  • Eukaryotic cytoplasmic ribosome → 80S
  • Made mainly of rRNA and proteins.
🕸️ CELL STRUCTURE

Cytoskeleton

The cytoskeleton is a network of protein fibres present in the cytoplasm.

  • Maintains cell shape.
  • Provides mechanical support.
  • Helps in cell movement.
  • Helps in intracellular transport.
🏊 CELL MOVEMENT

Cilia and Flagella

Cilia

Short, numerous structures that help in movement of the cell or substances over its surface.

Flagella

Usually longer structures that help in locomotion of cells.

🔵 ORGANELLE

Centrosome and Centrioles

The centrosome is commonly found in animal cells and contains a pair of centrioles.

Centrioles participate in the organisation of spindle fibres during cell division.

🧠 ORGANELLE

Nucleus

The nucleus contains the genetic material of the cell and controls many cellular activities.

Main Components

  • Nuclear envelope
  • Nucleoplasm
  • Nucleolus
  • Chromatin

Chromatin contains DNA associated with proteins.

📊 COMPARISON

Prokaryotic vs Eukaryotic Cells

Feature Prokaryotic Eukaryotic
Nucleus No true nucleus True nucleus present
Membrane-bound organelles Absent Present
DNA Mainly circular Mainly linear chromosomes
Ribosomes 70S 80S in cytoplasm
Examples Bacteria Plants and animals
🌿🐾 COMPARISON

Plant Cell vs Animal Cell

Feature Plant Cell Animal Cell
Cell wall Present Absent
Plastids Present in photosynthetic cells Absent
Vacuole Usually large central vacuole Usually smaller vacuoles
Centrioles Generally absent in higher plants Present
⚡ QUICK REVISION

Important Points

  • Cell → basic structural and functional unit of life.
  • Prokaryotic cell → no true nucleus.
  • Eukaryotic cell → membrane-bound nucleus.
  • Ribosome → protein synthesis.
  • Mitochondria → major site of ATP production.
  • Chloroplast → photosynthesis.
  • Golgi apparatus → modification and packaging.
  • Lysosome → intracellular digestion.
  • Rough ER → associated with protein synthesis.
  • Smooth ER → associated with lipid synthesis.
  • Nucleus → contains genetic material.
  • Cell membrane → selectively permeable boundary.
📝 PRACTICE

Important Questions

Q1. What is the basic unit of life?

The cell.

Q2. What is the function of ribosomes?

Protein synthesis.

Q3. Which organelle is called the powerhouse of the cell?

Mitochondrion.

Q4. What is the main function of Golgi apparatus?

Modification, packaging and transport of cellular materials.

Q5. What is the difference between prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells?

Prokaryotic cells lack a true membrane-bound nucleus, whereas eukaryotic cells have one.
