Class 11 Biology

🌱 Respiration in Plants

Complete chapter notes covering glycolysis, fermentation, aerobic respiration, Krebs cycle, ETS, ATP production, respiratory quotient and important NCERT concepts.

📖 Chapter Overview

Respiration is the process by which living organisms obtain energy from food. In plants, glucose is broken down through a series of biochemical reactions and energy is released mainly in the form of ATP.

Important: Cellular respiration may occur through aerobic respiration in the presence of oxygen or through fermentation under anaerobic conditions.

⚡ 1. Cellular Respiration

Cellular respiration is a catabolic process in which organic molecules are broken down to release energy.

Overall aerobic respiration

Glucose + Oxygen → Carbon dioxide + Water + Energy

The released energy is conserved in the form of ATP.

🔬 2. Types of Respiration

Type Oxygen Main Products
Aerobic respiration Required CO₂ + H₂O
Fermentation Not required Alcohol/Acid + CO₂

🧪 3. Glycolysis

Glycolysis is the breakdown of one molecule of glucose into two molecules of pyruvate.

  • Occurs in the cytoplasm.
  • Does not directly require oxygen.
  • One glucose molecule forms two pyruvate molecules.
  • It is common to both aerobic respiration and fermentation.

Important sequence

Glucose → Fructose-1,6-bisphosphate → 2 Pyruvate

Net products of glycolysis

  • 2 Pyruvate
  • 2 ATP (net)
  • 2 NADH

🍞 4. Fermentation

Fermentation is an anaerobic process in which glucose is incompletely broken down.

Alcoholic fermentation

Commonly occurs in yeast.

Pyruvate → Acetaldehyde → Ethanol + CO₂

Lactic acid fermentation

Pyruvate can be converted into lactic acid under anaerobic conditions.

Fermentation produces much less ATP than complete aerobic oxidation of glucose.

🚪 5. Fate of Pyruvate

The fate of pyruvate depends on the availability of oxygen and the organism's metabolic conditions.

Condition Pathway
Oxygen available Aerobic respiration
Anaerobic in yeast Alcoholic fermentation
Anaerobic conditions Lactic acid fermentation

🏭 6. Aerobic Respiration

Aerobic respiration involves the complete oxidation of glucose in the presence of oxygen.

Major stages include:

  1. Glycolysis
  2. Formation of Acetyl-CoA
  3. Krebs cycle
  4. Electron Transport System
  5. Oxidative phosphorylation

🔄 7. Link Reaction

Pyruvate enters the mitochondrial matrix and undergoes oxidative decarboxylation to form Acetyl-CoA.

Pyruvate → Acetyl-CoA + CO₂ + NADH

This reaction connects glycolysis with the Krebs cycle.

♻️ 8. Krebs Cycle

The Krebs cycle, also called the citric acid cycle or TCA cycle, occurs in the mitochondrial matrix.

Acetyl-CoA enters the cycle and undergoes a series of reactions.

Major products per turn

  • 3 NADH
  • 1 FADH₂
  • 1 ATP/GTP
  • 2 CO₂

Since one glucose produces two Acetyl-CoA molecules, the cycle turns twice per glucose molecule.

⚙️ 9. Electron Transport System (ETS)

The electron transport system is located in the inner mitochondrial membrane.

NADH and FADH₂ donate electrons to the electron transport chain.

  • Electrons pass through electron carriers.
  • Energy is released during electron transfer.
  • Protons are pumped across the inner membrane.
  • A proton gradient is generated.
  • ATP synthase uses the proton gradient to form ATP.

🔋 10. Oxidative Phosphorylation

ATP formation associated with electron transfer through the electron transport system is called oxidative phosphorylation.

ADP + Pi → ATP

Oxygen acts as the final electron acceptor and is reduced to form water.

🧬 11. Chemiosmotic Hypothesis

The chemiosmotic hypothesis explains ATP formation using a proton gradient across the inner mitochondrial membrane.

  1. Electron transport releases energy.
  2. Protons are pumped across the membrane.
  3. A proton gradient develops.
  4. Protons move back through ATP synthase.
  5. ATP is formed from ADP and Pi.

🧮 12. ATP Yield

Complete oxidation of one glucose molecule produces ATP through substrate-level phosphorylation and oxidative phosphorylation.

ATP yield depends on the cellular conditions and shuttle systems involved in transferring reducing equivalents into mitochondria.

In modern biochemical accounting, the theoretical maximum is commonly described as about 30–32 ATP per glucose in eukaryotic cells.

🌡️ 13. Respiratory Quotient (RQ)

Respiratory quotient is the ratio of the volume of carbon dioxide evolved to the volume of oxygen consumed.

RQ = CO₂ evolved / O₂ consumed

Examples

Respiratory substrate Approximate RQ
Carbohydrates 1
Fats Less than 1
Organic acids Greater than 1

🌿 14. Respiration in Plants

  • Plants exchange gases through stomata, lenticels and root surfaces.
  • Every living plant cell requires energy.
  • Respiration occurs continuously in living cells.
  • Different plant tissues may have different respiratory rates.
  • Temperature, oxygen availability and substrate affect respiration.

🔄 15. Amphibolic Pathway

Respiration is considered an amphibolic pathway because respiratory intermediates can participate in both catabolic and anabolic reactions.

Thus, respiration is not only involved in energy production but also provides intermediates for biosynthetic processes.

📊 16. Aerobic Respiration vs Fermentation

Feature Aerobic Respiration Fermentation
Oxygen Required Not required
Glucose breakdown Complete Incomplete
End products CO₂ + H₂O Alcohol/organic acid + other products
ATP yield High Low

⚡ Quick Revision

  • Glycolysis occurs in the cytoplasm.
  • Glycolysis produces two pyruvate molecules from one glucose.
  • Pyruvate is converted into Acetyl-CoA during aerobic respiration.
  • Krebs cycle occurs in the mitochondrial matrix.
  • ETS is located in the inner mitochondrial membrane.
  • Oxygen is the final electron acceptor in aerobic respiration.
  • ATP synthase plays a key role in ATP formation.
  • Fermentation occurs without oxygen.
  • RQ = CO₂ evolved / O₂ consumed.
  • Respiration can provide intermediates for biosynthesis.
