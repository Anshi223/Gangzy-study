Animal Kingdom

Complete chapter notes, classification, important features and quick revision.

📖 CHAPTER OVERVIEW

Animal Kingdom

The animal kingdom includes multicellular, eukaryotic organisms that are generally heterotrophic and lack cell walls.

Animals show a great variety in body organisation, symmetry, germ layers, coelom, segmentation and systems of organs.

🔬 CLASSIFICATION

Bases of Animal Classification

1. Level of Organisation

Animals may have cellular, tissue, organ or organ-system level of organisation.

2. Symmetry

Animals may be asymmetrical, radially symmetrical or bilaterally symmetrical.

3. Germ Layers

Animals may be diploblastic or triploblastic depending on the number of embryonic germ layers.

4. Coelom

Based on the body cavity, animals may be acoelomate, pseudocoelomate or coelomate.

5. Segmentation

In some animals the body is divided into repeated segments.

6. Notochord

Presence or absence of a notochord helps distinguish chordates from non-chordates.

🧽 PHYLUM 1

Porifera

  • Commonly known as sponges.
  • Mostly marine animals.
  • Body has many pores called ostia.
  • Water canal system is present.
  • Mostly asymmetrical.
  • Cellular level of organisation.
  • Skeleton may contain spicules or spongin fibres.
  • Example: Sycon, Spongilla and Euspongia.
🌊 PHYLUM 2

Cnidaria

  • Mostly aquatic and marine.
  • Radially symmetrical.
  • Diploblastic animals.
  • Tissue level of organisation.
  • Have specialised stinging cells called cnidoblasts.
  • Gastrovascular cavity is present.
  • Forms include polyp and medusa.
  • Examples: Hydra, Aurelia and Adamsia.
🌊 PHYLUM 3

Ctenophora

  • Commonly called comb jellies.
  • Exclusively marine.
  • Radial symmetry.
  • Diploblastic animals.
  • Have eight rows of ciliated comb plates.
  • Bioluminescence is commonly seen.
  • Example: Pleurobrachia.
🪱 PHYLUM 4

Platyhelminthes

  • Commonly called flatworms.
  • Body is dorsoventrally flattened.
  • Bilateral symmetry.
  • Triploblastic animals.
  • Organ level of organisation.
  • Acoelomate animals.
  • Many are parasitic.
  • Examples: Taenia and Fasciola.
🪱 PHYLUM 5

Aschelminthes

  • Also called roundworms.
  • Bilateral symmetry.
  • Triploblastic animals.
  • Pseudocoelomate.
  • Organ-system level of organisation.
  • Digestive system is complete.
  • Examples: Ascaris and Wuchereria.
🪱 PHYLUM 6

Annelida

  • Body is divided into segments.
  • Bilateral symmetry.
  • Triploblastic animals.
  • True coelom is present.
  • Organ-system level of organisation.
  • Closed circulatory system.
  • Examples: Earthworm, Leech and Nereis.
🦋 PHYLUM 7

Arthropoda

  • Largest phylum of the animal kingdom.
  • Body is segmented.
  • Jointed appendages are present.
  • Chitinous exoskeleton is present.
  • Bilateral symmetry.
  • Open circulatory system.
  • Examples: Cockroach, Honey bee, Butterfly and Prawn.
🐚 PHYLUM 8

Mollusca

  • Mostly aquatic.
  • Soft, unsegmented body.
  • Body is generally divided into head, muscular foot and visceral mass.
  • Mantle is present.
  • Shell is commonly present.
  • Examples: Pila, Unio, Sepia and Octopus.
⭐ PHYLUM 9

Echinodermata

  • Exclusively marine.
  • Adults are radially symmetrical.
  • Larvae are bilaterally symmetrical.
  • Calcareous endoskeleton is present.
  • Water vascular system is present.
  • Tube feet help in locomotion and feeding.
  • Examples: Starfish and Sea urchin.
🌊 PHYLUM 10

Hemichordata

  • Marine animals.
  • Body is divided into proboscis, collar and trunk.
  • Gill slits are present.
  • Examples: Balanoglossus and Saccoglossus.
🐟 PHYLUM 11

Chordata

Main Characteristics

  • Notochord is present at least during some stage of development.
  • Dorsal hollow nerve cord is present.
  • Paired pharyngeal gill slits are present.
  • Post-anal tail is present.
  • Usually bilateral and triploblastic.
  • Organ-system level of organisation.
🐟 CHORDATA

Major Groups

Urochordata

Notochord is present mainly in the larval tail. Example: Ascidia.

Cephalochordata

Notochord extends from head to tail and persists throughout life. Example: Branchiostoma.

Vertebrata

Vertebral column and cranium are present.

🐠 VERTEBRATA

Major Vertebrate Classes

🐟 Cyclostomata

Jawless aquatic vertebrates. Examples include Petromyzon.

🐟 Chondrichthyes

Cartilaginous fishes. Examples include shark and ray.

🐠 Osteichthyes

Bony fishes. Examples include Rohu and Catla.

🐸 Amphibia

Live in aquatic as well as terrestrial environments. Examples include frog and toad.

🦎 Reptilia

Mostly terrestrial vertebrates with dry, cornified skin. Examples include lizard and snake.

🦅 Aves

Birds with feathers and forelimbs modified into wings.

🐘 Mammalia

Mammary glands are present. Most are viviparous. Examples include humans, cow and elephant.

⚡ QUICK REVISION

Remember These Points

  • Porifera → Canal system and pores.
  • Cnidaria → Cnidoblasts.
  • Ctenophora → Comb plates and bioluminescence.
  • Platyhelminthes → Flatworms and acoelomate.
  • Aschelminthes → Roundworms and pseudocoelomate.
  • Annelida → Segmentation and true coelom.
  • Arthropoda → Jointed appendages.
  • Mollusca → Soft body and mantle.
  • Echinodermata → Water vascular system.
  • Chordata → Notochord and dorsal hollow nerve cord.
📝 PRACTICE

Important Questions

Q1. What is the basis of animal classification?

Classification considers organisation, symmetry, germ layers, coelom, segmentation and notochord.

Q2. What is a coelom?

Coelom is a body cavity lined by mesoderm.

Q3. What is a notochord?

It is a supporting rod-like structure characteristic of chordates.

Q4. Which is the largest animal phylum?

Arthropoda.
