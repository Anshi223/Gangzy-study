Class 11 Biology

🌱 Plant Growth and Development

Complete chapter notes covering growth, development, differentiation, plant growth regulators, photoperiodism, vernalisation, dormancy and important concepts.

📖 Chapter Overview

Plant growth and development involve a series of coordinated processes through which a plant increases in size, develops new structures and undergoes differentiation.

Growth: An irreversible increase in size, mass or volume of a living organism or its parts.

🌿 1. Growth in Plants

Growth in plants is generally indeterminate because meristems continue to divide and produce new cells.

Characteristics of plant growth

  • Growth is generally irreversible.
  • It involves cell division.
  • It involves cell enlargement.
  • It involves cell differentiation.
  • Meristematic tissues are responsible for continuous growth.

🔬 2. Phases of Growth

Growth of a plant organ can be divided into three main phases.

Phase Main Event
Cell Division Formation of new cells through mitosis
Cell Elongation Increase in cell size
Cell Maturation Cells acquire specialised characteristics

📈 3. Growth Curve

When growth is plotted against time under suitable conditions, it often produces a sigmoid or S-shaped growth curve.

Three major phases

  1. Lag phase
  2. Log or exponential phase
  3. Stationary phase
Lag → Log/Exponential → Stationary

📏 4. Measurement of Growth

Growth can be measured using different parameters.

  • Increase in fresh weight
  • Increase in dry weight
  • Increase in length
  • Increase in area
  • Increase in volume
  • Increase in cell number

➕ 5. Arithmetic Growth

In arithmetic growth, after one daughter cell continues to divide while the other differentiates, the increase in size may occur at a constant rate.

Arithmetic growth → Constant increase with time

🚀 6. Geometric Growth

In geometric growth, both daughter cells retain the ability to divide, resulting in rapid increase in cell number.

Geometric growth → Rapid/exponential increase

🧬 7. Differentiation

Differentiation is the process by which cells become structurally and functionally specialised.

For example, meristematic cells differentiate into specialised tissues such as xylem, phloem and other permanent tissues.

🔄 8. Dedifferentiation

Dedifferentiation is the process in which mature, differentiated cells regain the capacity to divide under suitable conditions.

Example: Formation of secondary meristems from certain mature tissues.

🔁 9. Redifferentiation

Redifferentiation is the process in which cells formed through dedifferentiation again become specialised.

Differentiated cell → Dedifferentiation → Division → Redifferentiation

🌱 10. Development

Development includes all changes that occur during the life cycle of a plant.

It includes growth, differentiation and maturation.

Development = Growth + Differentiation + Maturation

🌿 11. Plant Growth Regulators

Plant growth regulators are small organic molecules that influence growth and development in plants.

Major groups

  • Auxins
  • Gibberellins
  • Cytokinins
  • Ethylene
  • Abscisic acid

🌿 12. Auxins

Auxins promote several aspects of plant growth and development.

Major functions

  • Promote cell elongation.
  • Help in apical dominance.
  • Promote root initiation at suitable concentrations.
  • Help in fruit development.
  • Can be used in rooting of cuttings.
  • May promote parthenocarpy in some plants.

🌾 13. Gibberellins

Gibberellins are important plant growth regulators involved in growth and development.

  • Promote stem elongation.
  • Promote bolting in rosette plants.
  • Help in seed germination.
  • Can promote fruit growth.
  • Help in breaking certain types of dormancy.

🌱 14. Cytokinins

Cytokinins promote cell division and influence several developmental processes.

  • Promote cell division.
  • Promote growth of lateral buds.
  • Delay leaf senescence.
  • Help in nutrient mobilisation.
  • Work together with auxins in tissue development.

🍎 15. Ethylene

Ethylene is a gaseous plant growth regulator.

  • Promotes fruit ripening.
  • Promotes senescence in many plant organs.
  • Promotes abscission.
  • Helps in responses to mechanical stress.
  • Can influence flowering in some plants.

🍂 16. Abscisic Acid (ABA)

Abscisic acid is commonly associated with growth inhibition and stress responses.

  • Promotes stomatal closure during water stress.
  • Helps maintain seed dormancy.
  • Inhibits growth in several situations.
  • Participates in plant stress responses.

☀️ 17. Photoperiodism

Photoperiodism is the response of plants to the relative duration of light and dark periods.

Flowering in many plants is influenced by the photoperiod.

Types of plants

Type Flowering Response
Short-day plants Flower when day length is below a critical value.
Long-day plants Flower when day length is above a critical value.
Day-neutral plants Flowering is not strongly controlled by photoperiod.

❄️ 18. Vernalisation

Vernalisation is the promotion of flowering by exposure to a period of low temperature.

It is important in some plants that require a cold period before flowering.

Low temperature treatment → Promotes flowering

🌰 19. Seed Dormancy

Seed dormancy is a condition in which a viable seed does not germinate even when environmental conditions may appear favourable.

Dormancy can help seeds survive unfavourable periods and ensures germination at an appropriate time.

💧 20. Environmental Factors Affecting Growth

  • Light
  • Temperature
  • Water
  • Oxygen
  • Mineral nutrients
  • Carbon dioxide availability

Plant hormones and environmental factors interact to control growth and development.

📊 21. Major Plant Growth Regulators – Quick Table

Regulator Important Role
Auxin Cell elongation, apical dominance, rooting
Gibberellin Stem elongation, bolting, germination
Cytokinin Cell division, delay of senescence
Ethylene Fruit ripening, senescence, abscission
ABA Growth inhibition, dormancy, stress response

⚡ Quick Revision

  • Growth involves cell division, elongation and maturation.
  • Sigmoid growth curve has lag, log and stationary phases.
  • Differentiation produces specialised cells.
  • Dedifferentiation restores the ability to divide.
  • Redifferentiation produces specialised cells again.
  • Auxins promote cell elongation and apical dominance.
  • Gibberellins promote stem elongation and bolting.
  • Cytokinins promote cell division and delay senescence.
  • Ethylene is a gaseous plant growth regulator.
  • ABA is associated with dormancy and stress responses.
  • Photoperiodism is the response to light-dark duration.
  • Vernalisation involves low-temperature treatment.
  • Seed dormancy prevents germination under certain conditions.
