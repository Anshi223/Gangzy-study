Class 11 Biology • Chapter 18

🧠 Neural Control and Coordination

Complete notes covering neurons, nerves, human nervous system, central nervous system, peripheral nervous system, visceral nervous system and generation and conduction of nerve impulses.

📖 Chapter Overview

The nervous system controls and coordinates various activities of the body. It receives information from the surroundings, processes it and produces appropriate responses.

Stimulus → Receptor → Nervous system → Response

🧠 1. Nervous System

The nervous system is responsible for rapid communication, control and coordination in the body.

Major functions

  • Receives sensory information.
  • Processes information.
  • Coordinates body activities.
  • Produces appropriate responses.
  • Helps maintain internal coordination.

🔬 2. Neuron

A neuron is the structural and functional unit of the nervous system.

Main parts

  • Cell body or cyton
  • Dendrites
  • Axon
Dendrites → Receive signals
Cell body → Integrates cellular functions
Axon → Conducts nerve impulse away from cell body

🌿 3. Dendrites

Dendrites are branched extensions of a neuron that generally receive signals from receptors or other neurons.

➡️ 4. Axon

The axon is a long process that carries nerve impulses away from the cell body.

The terminal branches of an axon form specialised endings that communicate with other cells.

🧬 5. Myelin Sheath

Some axons are covered by a myelin sheath that electrically insulates the axon and helps increase the speed of impulse conduction.

Myelin sheath → Faster conduction of nerve impulses

⚡ 6. Nerve Impulse

A nerve impulse is an electrical signal that travels along a neuron.

It results from changes in the electrical state of the neuron membrane caused by movement of ions across the membrane.

⚡ 7. Resting Membrane Potential

When a neuron is not conducting an impulse, its membrane is in a polarised state.

The inside of the neuron is relatively negative compared with the outside.

Resting neuron → Polarised membrane

⚡ 8. Action Potential

When a sufficiently strong stimulus reaches a neuron, the membrane undergoes rapid changes in electrical potential. This produces an action potential.

Basic sequence

Stimulus → Depolarisation → Repolarisation → Recovery

➕ 9. Depolarisation

During depolarisation, the membrane potential becomes less negative and may become positive because of ion movements across the membrane.

Sodium ion movement into the neuron is an important event during the rising phase of an action potential.

➖ 10. Repolarisation

After depolarisation, the membrane potential moves back towards its resting condition.

Potassium ion movement out of the neuron contributes significantly to repolarisation.

🔄 11. Conduction of Nerve Impulse

An action potential at one region of the axon causes adjacent regions to become excited, allowing the impulse to propagate along the axon.

Action potential → Adjacent membrane excited → Impulse moves forward

⚡ 12. Saltatory Conduction

In myelinated axons, the myelin sheath covers most of the axon while small gaps called nodes of Ranvier remain exposed.

The impulse effectively jumps from one node to the next, making conduction much faster.

Myelinated axon → Node to node conduction → Faster impulse

🔗 13. Synapse

A synapse is a functional junction between two neurons or between a neuron and an effector cell.

Signals can be transmitted from one neuron to another through chemical or electrical mechanisms.

🧪 14. Chemical Synapse

At a chemical synapse, the arrival of an action potential at the axon terminal causes release of neurotransmitter molecules.

Action potential → Neurotransmitter release → Synaptic cleft → Receptor on next cell

🧠 15. Neurotransmitters

Neurotransmitters are chemical messengers released by neurons to communicate with other cells.

They bind to receptors on the postsynaptic membrane and can either excite or inhibit the target cell depending on the receptor and neurotransmitter system.

🧠 16. Human Nervous System

The human nervous system can be broadly divided into:

  • Central nervous system (CNS)
  • Peripheral nervous system (PNS)
  • Visceral nervous system
Nervous system = CNS + PNS + Visceral nervous system

🧠 17. Central Nervous System

The central nervous system consists of the:

  • Brain
  • Spinal cord

It receives and processes information and coordinates responses.

🧠 18. Brain

The brain is the major control centre of the nervous system. It receives sensory information and coordinates responses.

Major regions

  • Forebrain
  • Midbrain
  • Hindbrain

🧠 19. Forebrain

The forebrain contains important regions involved in sensory processing, voluntary activities, memory, learning and higher mental functions.

Important structures

  • Cerebrum
  • Thalamus
  • Hypothalamus

🧠 20. Cerebrum

The cerebrum is the largest part of the human brain and is responsible for many higher functions.

  • Thinking
  • Memory
  • Learning
  • Reasoning
  • Voluntary motor activities
  • Interpretation of sensory information

🧠 21. Thalamus

The thalamus acts as an important relay centre for sensory information travelling to the cerebral cortex.

🌡️ 22. Hypothalamus

The hypothalamus helps regulate several important homeostatic functions.

  • Body temperature
  • Hunger
  • Thirst
  • Water balance
  • Sleep-related functions
  • Endocrine regulation

🧠 23. Midbrain

The midbrain forms an important part of the brainstem and participates in the integration of certain visual and auditory responses.

🧠 24. Hindbrain

The hindbrain includes:

  • Cerebellum
  • Pons
  • Medulla oblongata

⚖️ 25. Cerebellum

The cerebellum plays an important role in coordination of muscular activity, posture and balance.

Cerebellum → Coordination + Balance + Posture

🌉 26. Pons

The pons is part of the brainstem and helps connect different regions of the brain. It also participates in regulation of respiratory activity.

🫁 27. Medulla Oblongata

The medulla contains centres involved in the regulation of several involuntary functions.

  • Breathing
  • Cardiac activity
  • Blood vessel activity
  • Other visceral functions

🦴 28. Spinal Cord

The spinal cord extends from the brainstem through the vertebral column.

Functions

  • Conducts nerve impulses between brain and body.
  • Acts as an important centre for reflex activities.

🧠 29. Peripheral Nervous System

The peripheral nervous system consists mainly of nerves and ganglia outside the brain and spinal cord.

It connects the central nervous system with receptors, muscles and glands.

🔌 30. Somatic Nervous System

The somatic nervous system is associated mainly with sensory information and voluntary control of skeletal muscles.

🫀 31. Visceral Nervous System

The visceral nervous system regulates many involuntary functions of internal organs.

It is closely associated with the autonomic nervous system.

🔄 32. Basic Pathway of Neural Coordination

Stimulus ↓ Receptor ↓ Sensory neuron ↓ CNS ↓ Motor neuron ↓ Effector ↓ Response

📊 33. CNS vs PNS

Feature CNS PNS
Major components Brain and spinal cord Nerves and ganglia
Main role Processing and coordination Connects CNS with body

📊 34. Dendrite vs Axon

Feature Dendrite Axon
General direction Towards cell body Away from cell body
Structure Usually short and branched Usually long and may be branched at terminal end
Main role Receives signals Conducts signals

⚡ Quick Revision

  • Neuron is the structural and functional unit of the nervous system.
  • Dendrites generally receive signals.
  • Axon generally carries impulses away from the cell body.
  • Myelin sheath increases the speed of impulse conduction.
  • Nodes of Ranvier occur between myelinated segments.
  • Saltatory conduction occurs in myelinated axons.
  • Synapse is a functional junction between neurons or a neuron and effector.
  • CNS consists of brain and spinal cord.
  • PNS connects the CNS with the rest of the body.
  • Cerebrum is associated with higher mental functions.
  • Thalamus acts as an important sensory relay centre.
  • Hypothalamus helps maintain homeostasis.
  • Cerebellum coordinates movement and balance.
  • Medulla regulates several involuntary functions.
  • Spinal cord conducts impulses and participates in reflexes.
