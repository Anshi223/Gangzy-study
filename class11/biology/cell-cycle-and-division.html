Cell Cycle and Cell Division

Complete notes on cell cycle, interphase, mitosis, meiosis and important stages of cell division.

📖 CHAPTER OVERVIEW

Cell Cycle and Cell Division

Cell division is an essential process by which new cells are produced from pre-existing cells.

The cell cycle includes the period of cell growth and preparation followed by cell division.

🔄 CONCEPT 1

Cell Cycle

The cell cycle is the sequence of events through which a cell passes between two successive cell divisions.

Main Phases

  • Interphase
  • M Phase

Interphase includes G₁, S and G₂ phases. The M phase includes nuclear division and cytokinesis.

1️⃣ G₁ PHASE

G₁ Phase

  • Cell grows in size.
  • Cellular metabolism is active.
  • Proteins and RNA are synthesised.
  • Cell prepares for DNA replication.
2️⃣ S PHASE

S Phase

During the S phase, DNA replication takes place.

  • DNA content doubles.
  • Chromosome number remains unchanged.
  • Histone proteins are synthesised.
  • Chromosomes become duplicated.
3️⃣ G₂ PHASE

G₂ Phase

  • Cell continues to grow.
  • Proteins required for cell division are produced.
  • Cell prepares for mitosis or meiosis.
4️⃣ M PHASE

M Phase

The M phase represents the actual cell division. It includes nuclear division followed by cytokinesis.

  • Karyokinesis → division of nucleus.
  • Cytokinesis → division of cytoplasm.
🧫 CELL DIVISION 1

Mitosis

Mitosis is an equational division in which chromosomes are distributed so that daughter cells generally retain the same chromosome number as the parent cell.

It is important for growth, development, tissue maintenance and asexual reproduction in some organisms.

🔬 MITOSIS — STAGE 1

Prophase

  • Chromatin condenses into visible chromosomes.
  • Each chromosome consists of two sister chromatids.
  • Nucleolus gradually disappears.
  • Spindle apparatus begins to form.
  • Nuclear envelope breaks down toward the end of prophase.
🔬 MITOSIS — STAGE 2

Metaphase

  • Chromosomes arrange at the equatorial plate.
  • Spindle fibres attach to chromosome regions.
  • Chromosomes are highly condensed and easily visible.
🔬 MITOSIS — STAGE 3

Anaphase

  • Sister chromatids separate.
  • Separated chromatids move toward opposite poles.
  • Each separated chromatid becomes a daughter chromosome.
🔬 MITOSIS — STAGE 4

Telophase

  • Chromosomes reach opposite poles.
  • Chromosomes begin to decondense.
  • Nuclear envelopes reform.
  • Nucleoli reappear.
✂️ CELL DIVISION

Cytokinesis

Animal Cells

Cytokinesis occurs by formation of a cleavage furrow that eventually separates the two daughter cells.

Plant Cells

Cytokinesis generally occurs through formation of a cell plate between the daughter nuclei.

🧬 CELL DIVISION 2

Meiosis

Meiosis is a specialised type of cell division associated with the formation of haploid cells.

It consists of two successive divisions: Meiosis I and Meiosis II.

🧬 MEIOSIS I

First Meiotic Division

Meiosis I is the reductional division because homologous chromosomes separate from each other.

Stages

  • Prophase I
  • Metaphase I
  • Anaphase I
  • Telophase I
⭐ IMPORTANT

Prophase I

Prophase I is the longest and most complex stage of meiosis.

Five Sub-stages

Leptotene

Chromosomes begin to condense and become visible.

Zygotene

Homologous chromosomes pair by synapsis.

Pachytene

Crossing over occurs between non-sister chromatids of homologous chromosomes.

Diplotene

Homologous chromosomes begin separating but remain connected at chiasmata.

Diakinesis

Chromosomes become highly condensed and prepare for metaphase I.

🔬 MEIOSIS I

Metaphase I

  • Homologous chromosome pairs arrange at the equator.
  • Spindle fibres attach to chromosome regions.
🔬 MEIOSIS I

Anaphase I

Homologous chromosomes separate and move toward opposite poles.

Sister chromatids remain together.

🔬 MEIOSIS I

Telophase I

Chromosomes reach the poles and the cell may undergo cytokinesis.

The resulting cells have a haploid chromosome set.

🧬 MEIOSIS II

Second Meiotic Division

Meiosis II resembles mitosis because sister chromatids separate during anaphase II.

Stages

  • Prophase II
  • Metaphase II
  • Anaphase II
  • Telophase II
🔬 MEIOSIS II

Stages of Meiosis II

Prophase II

Chromosomes condense and a new spindle apparatus forms.

Metaphase II

Chromosomes arrange at the equatorial plate.

Anaphase II

Sister chromatids separate and move toward opposite poles.

Telophase II

Nuclear envelopes form around the chromosome sets followed by cytokinesis.

⭐ NEET IMPORTANT

Crossing Over

Crossing over is the exchange of genetic material between non-sister chromatids of homologous chromosomes.

It occurs during the pachytene stage of prophase I and contributes to genetic variation.

📊 COMPARISON

Mitosis vs Meiosis

Feature Mitosis Meiosis
Number of divisions One Two
Chromosome number Generally maintained Reduced in daughter cells
Crossing over Normally absent Occurs during Prophase I
Major role Growth and cell replacement Formation of haploid cells and variation
Daughter cells Usually two Usually four
⭐ IMPORTANCE

Significance of Cell Division

Growth

Cell division increases the number of cells during growth and development.

Repair

Cell division helps replace damaged or worn-out cells in many tissues.

Reproduction

Cell division is involved in reproduction and formation of reproductive cells.

Genetic Variation

Meiosis and crossing over contribute to genetic variation.

⚡ QUICK REVISION

Must Remember

  • Cell cycle → Interphase + M phase.
  • Interphase → G₁ + S + G₂.
  • S phase → DNA replication.
  • M phase → nuclear division + cytokinesis.
  • Mitosis → one division.
  • Meiosis → two successive divisions.
  • Prophase I → longest and most complex stage.
  • Zygotene → synapsis.
  • Pachytene → crossing over.
  • Diplotene → chiasmata become visible.
  • Anaphase I → homologous chromosomes separate.
  • Anaphase II → sister chromatids separate.
  • Plant cytokinesis → cell plate.
  • Animal cytokinesis → cleavage furrow.
📝 PRACTICE

Important Questions

Q1. What are the phases of interphase?

G₁, S and G₂ phases.

Q2. In which phase does DNA replication occur?

S phase of interphase.

Q3. What happens during anaphase I?

Homologous chromosomes separate and move toward opposite poles.

Q4. During which stage does crossing over occur?

Pachytene stage of Prophase I.

Q5. What is cytokinesis?

Division of the cytoplasm following nuclear division.
