Class 11 Biology

🍽️ Digestion and Absorption

Complete notes on the human digestive system, digestive glands, enzymes, digestion, absorption, assimilation and digestive disorders.

📖 Chapter Overview

Food provides energy and materials required for growth, maintenance and repair. Complex food substances must be converted into simpler absorbable forms before they can be used by the body.

Digestion → Breakdown of complex food into simpler substances that can be absorbed and utilised by the body.

🧑‍⚕️ 1. Human Digestive System

The human digestive system consists of the alimentary canal and associated digestive glands.

Alimentary canal

  • Mouth
  • Buccal cavity
  • Pharynx
  • Oesophagus
  • Stomach
  • Small intestine
  • Large intestine
  • Rectum
  • Anus
Mouth → Pharynx → Oesophagus → Stomach → Small Intestine → Large Intestine → Rectum → Anus

👄 2. Buccal Cavity

The mouth opens into the buccal cavity. It contains teeth, tongue and salivary glands.

Functions

  • Ingestion of food
  • Chewing and mechanical breakdown
  • Mixing food with saliva
  • Beginning of carbohydrate digestion

Saliva

Saliva contains salivary amylase, which begins the digestion of starch.

🦷 3. Teeth

Teeth help in the mechanical breakdown of food.

Type Main Function
Incisors Cutting
Canines Tearing
Premolars Crushing and grinding
Molars Grinding

👅 4. Tongue

The tongue is a muscular organ present in the buccal cavity.

  • Helps in mixing food with saliva.
  • Helps in swallowing.
  • Contains taste receptors.
  • Helps form the food bolus.

🧪 5. Stomach

The stomach is a muscular organ located between the oesophagus and small intestine.

Gastric juice contains

  • Hydrochloric acid (HCl)
  • Pepsinogen
  • Mucus

HCl provides an acidic medium and helps convert pepsinogen into active pepsin.

Pepsin → Digestion of proteins

🧫 6. Small Intestine

The small intestine is the major site of digestion and absorption.

It receives bile from the liver and pancreatic juice from the pancreas.

Major parts

  • Duodenum
  • Jejunum
  • Ileum

🟢 7. Liver and Bile

The liver is the largest gland of the human body. It produces bile.

Functions of bile

  • Helps neutralise acidic chyme.
  • Provides an alkaline medium for intestinal enzymes.
  • Emulsifies fats.
Bile does not contain digestive enzymes.

🟡 8. Pancreas

The pancreas produces pancreatic juice containing several digestive enzymes.

Enzyme Acts Mainly On
Pancreatic amylase Carbohydrates
Trypsin Proteins/peptides
Lipase Fats

🧪 9. Intestinal Juice

The intestinal glands secrete intestinal juice, which contains enzymes involved in the final stages of digestion.

  • Completes carbohydrate digestion.
  • Completes protein digestion.
  • Helps convert nutrients into absorbable forms.

🍚 10. Digestion of Carbohydrates

Carbohydrates are ultimately converted into simple sugars, mainly glucose and other monosaccharides.

Complex carbohydrates → Simple sugars → Absorption

🥚 11. Digestion of Proteins

Proteins are broken down into smaller peptides and finally into amino acids.

Proteins → Peptides → Amino acids

🥑 12. Digestion of Fats

Bile salts help emulsify fats into smaller droplets, increasing the surface area available for lipase.

Lipase breaks fats into simpler products including fatty acids and glycerol.

Fats → Fatty acids + Glycerol

➡️ 13. Peristalsis

Peristalsis is the rhythmic contraction and relaxation of the muscles of the alimentary canal that helps move food forward.

Peristalsis → Forward movement of food through the alimentary canal

🧬 14. Absorption

Absorption is the process by which digested nutrients pass from the digestive tract into the blood or lymph.

Site of maximum absorption

The small intestine is the major site of absorption.

🔬 15. Villi

The inner surface of the small intestine contains numerous finger-like projections called villi.

Importance of villi

  • Increase surface area for absorption.
  • Contain blood capillaries.
  • Contain lymphatic vessels called lacteals.
Villi → Large surface area → Efficient absorption

🩸 16. Absorption of Nutrients

Nutrient Major Absorbable Form
Carbohydrates Monosaccharides
Proteins Amino acids
Fats Fatty acids and monoglycerides
Water Water
Minerals Ions
Vitamins Various forms

🔄 17. Assimilation

Assimilation is the utilisation of absorbed nutrients by body cells for energy, growth, repair and other metabolic activities.

Digestion → Absorption → Assimilation

🚽 18. Large Intestine

The large intestine receives undigested material from the small intestine.

  • Absorbs water and electrolytes.
  • Helps form faeces.
  • Stores faecal matter before elimination.

➡️ 19. Egestion

Egestion is the elimination of undigested and unabsorbed food material from the digestive tract through the anus.

⚠️ 20. Digestive Disorders

Indigestion

Indigestion refers to discomfort associated with digestion.

Constipation

Constipation involves difficulty or infrequent passage of stools.

Diarrhoea

Diarrhoea involves frequent passage of loose or watery stools.

Vomiting

Vomiting is the forceful expulsion of stomach contents through the mouth.

Jaundice

Jaundice is associated with yellowing of the skin and eyes due to increased bilirubin levels.

Protein-Energy Malnutrition (PEM)

PEM results from inadequate intake of protein and energy and can seriously affect growth and health.

📊 21. Quick Digestive Enzyme Revision

Enzyme Substrate Product/Role

Pancreatic amylase Starch Smaller carbohydrates

⚡ Quick Revision

  • Digestion converts complex food into simpler absorbable substances.
  • The alimentary canal starts at the mouth and ends at the anus.
  • Salivary amylase begins carbohydrate digestion.
  • Pepsin acts on proteins in the stomach.
  • Bile is produced by the liver.
  • Bile helps in emulsification of fats.
  • The pancreas secretes pancreatic digestive enzymes.
  • Small intestine is the major site of digestion and absorption.
  • Villi increase the absorptive surface area.
  • Assimilation is the utilisation of absorbed nutrients.
  • Egestion removes undigested material.
