Structural Organisation in Animals

Complete notes on animal tissues, organ systems and important examples including earthworm, cockroach and frog.

📖 CHAPTER OVERVIEW

Structural Organisation in Animals

In multicellular animals, cells are organised into tissues, tissues form organs, and organs work together as organ systems.

Animal tissues are broadly classified into epithelial, connective, muscular and nervous tissues.

🔬 PART 1

Animal Tissues

1. Epithelial Tissue

Forms the covering or lining of body surfaces and cavities.

2. Connective Tissue

Connects, supports, binds and protects different parts of the body.

3. Muscular Tissue

Responsible mainly for movement by contraction.

4. Nervous Tissue

Receives and transmits information through electrical and chemical signals.

🧱 TISSUE 1

Epithelial Tissue

Epithelial cells are closely packed and have very little intercellular material.

Functions

  • Protection
  • Absorption
  • Secretion
  • Excretion
  • Exchange of substances
🔬 EPITHELIAL TYPES

Types of Epithelial Tissue

Simple Squamous

Thin, flat cells. Suitable for diffusion and filtration. Example: lining of blood vessels.

Simple Cuboidal

Cube-shaped cells. Common in kidney tubules and glandular ducts.

Simple Columnar

Tall cells involved mainly in absorption and secretion. Common in the intestinal lining.

Ciliated Epithelium

Has cilia that help move substances across the epithelial surface.

Stratified Epithelium

Consists of multiple layers and is mainly protective.

Glandular Epithelium

Specialised for secretion.

🔗 TISSUE 2

Connective Tissue

Connective tissues have cells embedded in an extracellular matrix. They provide support, connection, protection and transport.

Areolar Tissue

Acts as packing tissue between structures and helps in tissue repair.

Adipose Tissue

Stores fat and provides insulation and cushioning.

Tendon

Connects muscle to bone and consists mainly of dense collagen fibres.

Ligament

Connects bone to bone and provides support to joints.

Cartilage

Provides flexible support. Found in structures such as nose, ear and trachea.

Bone

Hard connective tissue that provides support and protection.

Blood

Fluid connective tissue involved in transport, defence and regulation.

Lymph

Fluid connective tissue involved in transport and immune functions.

💪 TISSUE 3

Muscular Tissue

Skeletal Muscle

  • Striated.
  • Usually multinucleate.
  • Voluntary control.
  • Attached mainly to bones.

Smooth Muscle

  • Non-striated.
  • Usually single nucleus.
  • Involuntary control.
  • Found in walls of many internal organs.

Cardiac Muscle

  • Striated.
  • Branched fibres.
  • Usually involuntary.
  • Found in the heart.
🧠 TISSUE 4

Nervous Tissue

Nervous tissue is specialised for receiving stimuli and transmitting nerve impulses.

Neuron

  • Cell body
  • Dendrites
  • Axon

Neurons are supported by specialised cells known as neuroglia.

🪱 PART 2

Earthworm

Important Features

  • Belongs to phylum Annelida.
  • Body is elongated and segmented.
  • Segments are separated by grooves.
  • Setae help in locomotion.
  • Earthworm is hermaphroditic.
  • Clitellum is involved in reproduction.
  • Closed circulatory system is present.
  • Nephridia help in excretion.
🪱 EARTHWORM

Digestive System

The alimentary canal is a straight tube extending from the mouth to the anus.

Major regions include the pharynx, oesophagus, crop, gizzard, intestine and anus.

🪳 PART 3

Cockroach

Important Features

  • Belongs to phylum Arthropoda.
  • Body is divided into head, thorax and abdomen.
  • Has three pairs of walking legs.
  • Two pairs of wings are present.
  • Respiration occurs through tracheae.
  • Excretion occurs mainly through Malpighian tubules.
  • Sexes are separate.
🪳 COCKROACH

Digestive System

The alimentary canal consists of the foregut, midgut and hindgut.

  • Mouth
  • Pharynx
  • Oesophagus
  • Crop
  • Gizzard
  • Midgut
  • Hindgut
  • Anus
🫁 COCKROACH

Respiratory System

Respiration occurs through a network of tracheal tubes.

  • Spiracles open to the outside.
  • Tracheae branch into finer tracheoles.
  • Tracheoles deliver oxygen close to tissues.
🐸 PART 4

Frog

Important Features

  • Frog belongs to class Amphibia.
  • It can live in aquatic and terrestrial habitats.
  • Body is divided into head and trunk.
  • Forelimbs and hindlimbs are present.
  • Skin is moist and glandular.
  • Respiration can occur through skin and lungs.
  • Heart is three-chambered.
  • Fertilisation is generally external in frogs.
🐸 FROG

Digestive System

The digestive system consists of an alimentary canal and associated digestive glands.

  • Mouth
  • Buccal cavity
  • Pharynx
  • Oesophagus
  • Stomach
  • Small intestine
  • Large intestine
  • Cloaca
❤️ FROG

Circulatory System

  • Frog has a closed circulatory system.
  • Heart is three-chambered.
  • Two atria and one ventricle are present.
  • Circulation includes pulmonary and systemic components.

  • Ligament → bone to bone.

📝 PRACTICE

Important Questions

Q1. Name the four basic animal tissues.

Epithelial, connective, muscular and nervous tissues.

Q2. What connects muscle to bone?

Tendon.

Q3. What connects bone to bone?

Ligament.

Q4. How does a cockroach respire?

Through its tracheal respiratory system.

Q5. How many chambers does a frog's heart have?

Three chambers: two atria and one ventricle.
