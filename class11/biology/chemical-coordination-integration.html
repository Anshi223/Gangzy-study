🧪 Chemical Coordination and Integration

Class 11 Biology • Human Physiology

Learn endocrine glands, hormones, their functions and hormonal regulation in an easy revision-friendly format.

📌 Chapter Overview

Chemical coordination in the human body is mainly carried out by hormones. Hormones are chemical messengers released by endocrine glands and transported through the blood to their target tissues or organs.

  • Endocrine glands
  • Hormones
  • Hypothalamus
  • Pituitary gland
  • Thyroid and parathyroid glands
  • Adrenal glands
  • Pancreas
  • Gonads
  • Hormonal regulation

🧠 Endocrine System

The endocrine system consists of glands that release hormones directly into the blood.

Endocrine Glands

Endocrine glands are ductless glands. Their secretions are called hormones.

💡 Hormone = Chemical messenger produced in small amounts that regulates specific functions of the body.

🧠 Hypothalamus

The hypothalamus is a part of the forebrain and connects the nervous system with the endocrine system.

  • Produces releasing and inhibiting hormones.
  • Controls the activity of the pituitary gland.
  • Helps maintain homeostasis.
⭐ Hypothalamus → controls Pituitary → controls many endocrine functions.

🧠 Pituitary Gland

The pituitary gland is located at the base of the brain and is closely associated with the hypothalamus.

Important Hormones

Hormone Main Function
Growth Hormone (GH) Promotes growth of body tissues.
Thyroid Stimulating Hormone (TSH) Stimulates the thyroid gland.
Adrenocorticotropic Hormone (ACTH) Acts mainly on the adrenal cortex.
Follicle Stimulating Hormone (FSH) Helps regulate reproductive functions.
Luteinising Hormone (LH) Regulates important reproductive processes.
Prolactin Associated with milk production.

🦋 Thyroid Gland

The thyroid gland is located in the neck region.

Major Hormones

  • Thyroxine (T4)
  • Triiodothyronine (T3)
  • Calcitonin

Thyroid hormones help regulate metabolism, growth and development.

⚠️ Iodine is required for the synthesis of thyroid hormones.

🦴 Parathyroid Glands

Parathyroid glands produce parathyroid hormone (PTH).

  • Helps regulate calcium levels in the blood.
  • Participates in calcium and phosphate balance.

⚡ Adrenal Glands

Adrenal glands are located above the kidneys.

Adrenal Cortex

  • Produces corticosteroid hormones.
  • Helps regulate metabolism and electrolyte balance.

Adrenal Medulla

  • Produces adrenaline.
  • Produces noradrenaline.
  • Helps the body respond to stressful situations.
⚡ Adrenaline is commonly associated with the "fight-or-flight" response.

🍬 Pancreas

The endocrine part of the pancreas contains Islets of Langerhans.

Hormone Function
Insulin Helps lower blood glucose level.
Glucagon Helps increase blood glucose level.
🍬 Insulin and glucagon work together to maintain blood glucose homeostasis.

♂️ Testes

Testes function as reproductive organs as well as endocrine glands.

Testosterone is the major androgen produced by the testes.

  • Supports development of male reproductive functions.
  • Contributes to development of male secondary sexual characters.

♀️ Ovaries

Ovaries are female reproductive organs and also act as endocrine glands.

Main Hormones

  • Estrogens
  • Progesterone

These hormones regulate important aspects of the female reproductive system.

🔬 Other Hormone-Producing Organs

Organ Important Hormone Main Role
Heart ANF Helps regulate blood pressure and fluid balance.
Kidney Erythropoietin Stimulates formation of red blood cells.
Gastrointestinal tract Various hormones Helps regulate digestive activities.

🔄 Hormonal Regulation

Hormone secretion is often regulated through feedback mechanisms.

Negative Feedback

When the level of a hormone or its effect becomes sufficient, signals reduce further secretion.

🔄 Example: Hypothalamus → Pituitary → Target gland → Hormone production → Feedback control

⚡ Quick Revision

  • Endocrine glands are ductless glands.
  • Hormones act as chemical messengers.
  • Hypothalamus regulates pituitary activity.
  • Pituitary hormones regulate several endocrine functions.
  • Thyroid hormones are important for metabolism, growth and development.
  • PTH helps regulate blood calcium.
  • Adrenaline is associated with the fight-or-flight response.
  • Insulin decreases blood glucose.
  • Glucagon increases blood glucose.
  • Testes produce testosterone.
  • Ovaries produce estrogens and progesterone.
  • Hormonal regulation commonly involves feedback mechanisms.
