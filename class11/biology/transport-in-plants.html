CLASS 11 BIOLOGY

🌱 Transport in Plants

Complete chapter notes covering movement of water, minerals, food and other substances in plants.

📖 Chapter Overview

Plants continuously transport water, minerals, food and other substances from one part of the plant to another. Unlike animals, plants do not have a circulatory system like a heart and blood vessels. Instead, they use specialised tissues such as xylem and phloem.

💧 Water Transport

Water is mainly transported through xylem from roots towards aerial parts of the plant.

🧂 Mineral Transport

Mineral ions are absorbed mainly by roots and transported through the xylem.

🍃 Food Transport

Food produced during photosynthesis is transported through phloem to different parts of the plant.

1️⃣ Diffusion

Diffusion is the movement of molecules or ions from a region of higher concentration to a region of lower concentration.

Important Points

  • It is a passive process.
  • No metabolic energy is directly required.
  • Movement occurs along the concentration gradient.
  • Diffusion can occur in gases and liquids.
  • It is important in gas exchange in plants.

Factors Affecting Diffusion

  • Concentration gradient
  • Temperature
  • Distance
  • Density of the medium
  • Size of molecules

2️⃣ Facilitated Diffusion

Facilitated diffusion is the movement of substances across a membrane with the help of specific membrane proteins.

Key Features

  • Movement occurs down the concentration gradient.
  • Specific proteins are involved.
  • No direct expenditure of ATP is required.
  • It is selective.

3️⃣ Osmosis

Osmosis is the movement of water through a selectively permeable membrane from a region of higher water potential to a region of lower water potential.

Hypotonic Solution

Water enters the cell and the cell tends to become turgid.

Hypertonic Solution

Water moves out of the cell and the cell may undergo plasmolysis.

Isotonic Solution

There is no net movement of water across the membrane.

4️⃣ Water Potential

Water potential represents the tendency of water to move from one region to another.

Formula

Ψw = Ψs + Ψp

  • Ψw = Water potential
  • Ψs = Solute potential
  • Ψp = Pressure potential

Pure water has the highest water potential and its water potential is considered to be zero under standard conditions.

5️⃣ Plasmolysis

Plasmolysis is the shrinkage of the protoplast away from the cell wall when a plant cell loses water in a hypertonic solution.

Reverse Process

When a plasmolysed cell is placed in a hypotonic solution, water enters the cell and the cell becomes turgid again. This process is called deplasmolysis.

6️⃣ Active Transport

Active transport is the movement of substances across a membrane against their concentration gradient using metabolic energy.

  • Requires energy.
  • Usually involves specific transport proteins.
  • Can move substances against the concentration gradient.
  • Important for mineral ion uptake by roots.

7️⃣ Absorption of Water by Roots

Roots absorb water mainly through root hairs. Root hairs provide a large surface area for absorption.

Pathway of Water

Soil → Root Hair → Epidermis → Cortex → Endodermis → Pericycle → Xylem

Apoplast Pathway

In the apoplast pathway, water moves through cell walls and intercellular spaces without crossing plasma membranes.

Symplast Pathway

In the symplast pathway, water moves through the cytoplasm of cells connected by plasmodesmata.

⭐ Casparian Strips

Casparian strips are present in the endodermis and contain a water-impermeable substance called suberin. They regulate movement of water and minerals into the vascular tissues.

8️⃣ Xylem Transport

Xylem conducts water and dissolved minerals mainly from roots towards stems and leaves.

Tracheids

Elongated xylem cells that conduct water and provide support.

Vessels

Long tubular structures formed by vessel elements and specialised for water conduction.

9️⃣ Root Pressure

Root pressure is the positive pressure developed in the roots due to accumulation of ions and subsequent movement of water into the xylem.

Important

Root pressure can contribute to the movement of water, especially under conditions of low transpiration.

🔟 Transpiration

Transpiration is the loss of water in the form of water vapour from the aerial parts of plants, mainly through stomata.

Types of Transpiration

  • Stomatal transpiration – through stomata.
  • Cuticular transpiration – through cuticle.
  • Lenticular transpiration – through lenticels.

Importance of Transpiration

  • Creates transpiration pull.
  • Helps in upward movement of water.
  • Helps in mineral transport.
  • Provides cooling effect.
  • Helps maintain water movement through the plant.

1️⃣1️⃣ Transpiration Pull

Transpiration from leaves creates a negative pressure or tension in the xylem. This tension helps pull the continuous column of water upward from roots to leaves.

🌊 Cohesion-Tension Theory

The upward movement of water is explained mainly by the cohesion-tension theory.

  • Water molecules show cohesion.
  • Water molecules adhere to xylem walls.
  • Transpiration creates tension.
  • The continuous water column is pulled upward.

1️⃣2️⃣ Factors Affecting Transpiration

☀️ Light

Light generally promotes stomatal opening and increases transpiration.

🌡️ Temperature

Higher temperature generally increases evaporation and transpiration.

💨 Wind

Moderate wind can increase transpiration by removing water vapour around leaves.

💧 Humidity

High humidity generally decreases the rate of transpiration.

1️⃣3️⃣ Stomata

Stomata are tiny pores present mainly on leaves. Each stoma is surrounded by two guard cells.

Functions

  • Gas exchange.
  • Regulation of transpiration.
  • Entry of carbon dioxide for photosynthesis.
  • Release of oxygen and water vapour.

1️⃣4️⃣ Phloem Transport

Phloem transports organic food, especially sugars, from source regions to sink regions.

Source

A region where food is produced or becomes available for transport, such as mature photosynthetic leaves.

Sink

A region where food is consumed or stored, such as roots, fruits, seeds and growing tissues.

1️⃣5️⃣ Pressure Flow Hypothesis

The pressure flow hypothesis explains the translocation of sugars through phloem.

Sequence

  1. Sugars are loaded into the phloem at the source.
  2. Water enters the phloem from nearby xylem by osmosis.
  3. This creates positive pressure inside the phloem.
  4. The pressure drives phloem sap towards the sink.
  5. Sugars are unloaded at the sink.

📊 Xylem vs Phloem

Feature Xylem Phloem
Main function Water and mineral transport Food transport
Main direction Mostly upward Source to sink
Main conducting elements Tracheids and vessels Sieve tubes and companion cells
Energy requirement Bulk water movement is mainly passive Loading/unloading may require energy

⚡ Quick Revision

  • Diffusion = movement from higher concentration to lower concentration.
  • Osmosis = movement of water through a selectively permeable membrane.
  • Xylem transports water and minerals.
  • Phloem transports organic food.
  • Root hairs help in water absorption.
  • Casparian strips occur in the endodermis.
  • Transpiration mainly occurs through stomata.
  • Transpiration pull helps in upward movement of water.
  • Cohesion keeps water molecules together.
  • Phloem translocation occurs from source to sink.
  • Pressure flow hypothesis explains phloem translocation.
